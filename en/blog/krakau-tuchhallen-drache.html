Travel report

Magical Krakow: Cloth Hall, sweet revelations and a fire-breathing dragon

Poland · approx. 6 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Hello, this is Justine from Tussy Van! 💛 When you travel Poland, you lose your heart in many places - but Krakow (Kraków) has a very special magic. The former royal city exudes an elegance that you simply cannot resist. Here's an honest, up-close look at my absolute highlights - including a culinary insider tip that you won't find in any travel guide and a legendary dragon story!

The heart of the city: Main Market and Cloth Hall

The vibrant center of Krakow is the huge main market (Rynek Główny) — one of the largest medieval squares in Europe. Right in the middle are the beautiful Cloth Hall (Sukiennice). This Renaissance building is a dream for anyone who loves architecture and aesthetics.

In the halls and on the square in front you regularly find fantastic ones. seasonal markets instead. Whether it's an Easter, Christmas or autumn craft market - the atmosphere is always lively, colorful and full of anticipation. You stroll from stand to stand, admire handmade jewelry and wooden art and are magically drawn to the incredibly delicious scents.

My culinary insider tip: the sweet revelation of an older one Lady

Apart from the typical Polish pierogi, I made a discovery at the markets that was simply phenomenal. I can particularly recommend a very specific stand run by an older lady. She sells sweet specialties that are still making my mouth water:

  • The best baklava of my life: Many people know baklava as an extremely sweet, sticky affair. But here? Delicious and not at all overly sugary - with an absolutely perfect, subtle sweetness.
  • The unbeatable Napoleon cake: My absolute favorite! I have never eaten something so delicious in my entire life. The cream, the layers - a dream.
  • Sunflower seed halva: If you like halva, you definitely have to try this version with sunflower seeds. Incredibly tasty.

The best thing: Everything is completely homemade! Behind the scenes, a lady of Turkish or Arabic origin prepares the delicacies fresh every day and with a lot of love. And you can try a little bit of everything first - but I warn you: it doesn't just stop at trying. In the end you are guaranteed to move on with a full bag. An amazing experience!

The Wawel: Where kings resided and dragons lived

In Krakow the way up is part of the Wawel to the mandatory program The majestic palace and castle tower over the city and the Vistula and offer a wonderful view. The area is steeped in history, elegant and perfect for a long walk.

The most exciting highlight for young and old awaits down on the river bank, right at the foot of the hill: the famous fire-breathing dragon (Smok Wawelski) - a large bronze statue that actually breathes real fire every few minutes.

🐉 The legend from the Wawel Dragon: Many centuries ago, when King Krak ruled the city, a terrible dragon lived in a cave under Wawel Hill. He ate the farmers' livestock and demanded victims; many brave knights failed because of his fiery breath. There was a clever shoemaker boy named Skuba a brilliant idea: He filled a dead sheep with pure sulfur and placed it in front of the cave. The dragon greedily devoured the prey - and shortly afterwards the sulfur burned in its belly. Burning with thirst, he ran to the Vistula and drank and drank until it burst with a mighty bang. The city was saved, the little shoemaker boy became a hero - and Krakow celebrates its dragon to this day.

Conclusion

Krakow combines culture, lively market traditions and fairytale stories in a unique way. Whether you stroll through the Cloth Hall, try out the sweet sins of the older lady or watch the Wawel dragon breathe fire - this city is an absolute work of art that you should put on your wish list with a lot of anticipation!

By the way: Krakow was also a stage on my big 6-week road trip from Cologne across Europe. 🚌
