Magical Krakow: Cloth Halls, Sweet Revelations, and a Fire-Breathing Dragon

Poland · approx. 6 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Hello, this is Justine from Tussy Van! 💛 When you travel to Poland, you lose your heart to many places — but Krakow (Kraków) has a very special magic. The former royal city exudes an elegance you simply can't resist. Here's an honest, hands-on look into my absolute highlights — including a culinary insider tip you won't find in any travel guide, and a legendary dragon story!

The heart of the city: main market and cloth halls

The vibrant center of Krakow is the huge main market (Rynek Główny) — one of the largest medieval squares in Europe. Right in the middle, the beautiful Cloth halls (Sukiennice). This Renaissance building is a dream for anyone who loves architecture and aesthetics.

Fantastic performances regularly take place in the halls and on the square in front of them seasonal markets took place. Whether Easter, Christmas, or autumn craft market — the atmosphere is lively, colorful, and full of anticipation every time. You stroll from stall to stall, admire handmade jewelry and wood art, and are magically drawn to the incredibly delicious scents.

My culinary insider tip: the sweet revelation of an elderly lady

Away from the typical Polish pirogi, I made a discovery at the markets that was simply phenomenal. I can especially recommend a very specific stand of an older lady. She sells sweet specialties that still make my mouth water:

  • The best baklava of my life: Many know baklava as an extremely sweet, sticky affair. But here? Delicious and not over-sweetened at all — with an absolutely perfect, delicate sweetness.
  • The unbeatable Napoleon cake: My absolute favorite! I have never eaten anything so delicious in my entire life. The cream, the layers — a dream.
  • Sunflower Seed Halva: If you like halva, you absolutely have to try this version with sunflower seeds. Incredibly intensely flavorful.

The best part: Everything is completely homemade! Behind the scenes, a lady of Turkish or Arab descent prepares the delicacies fresh and with great love every day. And you can try a little of everything at first — but I warn you: it doesn't stop at just trying. In the end, you're guaranteed to move on with a full pocket. An incredible experience!

The Wawel: Where Kings Resided and Dragons Lived

In Krakow, the path up to the Wawel to the mandatory program. The majestic castle and fortress tower over the town and the Vistula, offering a beautiful view. The grounds are steeped in history, elegant, and perfect for an extensive walk.

But the most exciting highlight for young and old awaits down by the riverbank, right at the foot of the hill: the famous Fire-breathing dragon (Smok Wawelski) — a large bronze statue that actually spits real fire every few minutes.

🐉 The legend of the Wawel Dragon: Many centuries ago, when King Krak ruled the city, a terrible dragon lived in a cave beneath Wawel Hill. He ate the farmers' livestock and demanded sacrifices; many brave knights failed because of his fiery breath. There was a clever shoemaker's boy named Scuba a brilliant idea: He filled a dead sheep with pure sulfur and placed it in front of the cave. The dragon greedily devoured the prey — and shortly after, the sulfur burned in its belly. Burning with thirst, he ran to the Vistula and drank and drank until he burst with a tremendous bang. The city was saved, the little shoemaker's boy became a hero — and Krakow still celebrates its dragon today.

Conclusion

Krakow uniquely combines culture, vibrant market traditions, and fairytale stories. Whether you stroll through the cloth halls, try your way through the sweet sins of the elderly lady, or watch the Wawel dragon breathe fire — this city is an absolute total work of art that you should add to your wish list with great anticipation!

By the way: Krakow was also a stage on my big 6-week road trip from Cologne across Europe. 🚌
