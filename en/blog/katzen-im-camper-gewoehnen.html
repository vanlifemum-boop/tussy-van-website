Cats in the camper: How I get Timmy and Susi used to traveling

September 2026 · approx. 11 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Hello, this is Justine from Tussy Van! 💛

Anyone who sees my two cats quickly realizes: they are curious about In any case. Timmy and Susi are about one year old. The two Females are sisters from the same litter. Timmy wears longer gray-white fur, Susi is the short-haired white-gray sister. A open bag is inspected immediately, a new box within a few Seconds and at the window, the two could spend hours Observe what happens outside. Actually the best conditions for two little travel companions – right?

Of course, it's not quite that simple. Timmy and Susi should not suddenly put in the van and embarked on a long journey can be taken along. For cats, a change of location often means much more than for us humans. New smells, engine noises, movements, foreign People, dogs at the campsite, and an unusual daily routine can be exciting, but can also cause stress.

Therefore, I want to approach the topic slowly and honestly. My Goal is not to turn them into "Instagram camping cats" at any cost. I want to find out if they really feel comfortable in the camper can. Timmy and Susi set the pace. If one of the sisters clearly shows that traveling is not for them, then that is also one An answer I will respect.

First, the preparation, then the adventure

Before the first real tour begins, I have a Health check at the veterinarian. In doing so, I would like to clarify whether Timmy and Susi is physically fit for car rides, which vaccinations and which Parasite protection for our planned travel destinations makes sense and what I can do this in case of possible travel sickness. medications or I would never give tranquilizers on my own. If a If a cat is spitting heavily, vomiting, or panicking in the car, that's right? discussed with the veterinarian.

For foreign trips, the documents are included. Within the EU cats generally need a valid EU pet passport, a valid rabies vaccination and a unique microchip label. For a first rabies vaccination, the chip must be administered beforehand become; in addition, a waiting time of at least 21 days before the vaccination is recognized for travel. Important is also that the chip is not only set, but also in a Pet register with my current contact details. Before every trip across a border, I check the rules of the destination country and also the conditions for the return journey – especially in Non-EU countries.

Therefore, my folder includes:

  • EU pet passports and vaccination documents
  • Chip numbers and registration data
  • Contact details of my pet practice
  • Addresses of veterinary practices or veterinary clinics at the destination
  • Information on known diseases, allergies, and Medications
  • Recent photos of both cats for emergencies

Step 1: The transport box becomes your favorite spot

Preparation doesn't start in the van, but at home. Many cats You only see the transport box when you go to the vet. Then it will be fetched from the basement, put the cat inside, and after the appointment it disappears again. This way, the box can quickly reach the warning signal will be.

That's why we want the boxes to remain permanently open in the apartment stand. Each cat gets its own sturdy box with a familiar blanket. Treats, toys, or part of the daily Feed rations make the place interesting. At first, it is sufficient if the Cat looking at or sniffing the box. Later, they are rewarded when they enters, sits inside, and at some point briefly by the door aligned stay relaxed.

I won't force anything or push the cats in. I prefer to practice for two or three minutes a day and listen with a good Wait a minute. Only when the open box has become completely normal, I close the door for a few seconds, open it again and Slowly increase the time.

For the car ride, I plan two separate, sufficiently large Transport boxes. Timmy and Susi are littermates and sleep at home Happy to cuddle up together. Nevertheless, even familiar people Siblings react differently under stress. Separate boxes give both a protected area and prevent a restless cat from others are additionally pressured.

Step 2: Practice harness and leash without time pressure

A harness is not a substitute for the transport box during the ride. However, it can later be useful in a secure location. Therefore I would like to first give Timmy and Susi a well-placed Get used to escape-proof cat harnesses.

At first, the dishes simply lie next to the sleeping area. Then it briefly touches the back, later it is applied for a few seconds. Only when the cat moves normally, eats, and plays with it does it come the light leash to go with it. Pulling or pulling would be completely wrong Space. A cat does not automatically walk like a dog on foot – and it It doesn't have to be.

Whether Timmy and Susi want to go outside later remains to be seen. Maybe they'll enjoy a small, peaceful exploration round. Maybe they feel much more comfortable behind a secured window. Both are Alright. Outside, the dishes always remain under supervision; connected and alone, I wouldn't leave a single cat behind.

Step 3: The standing one Discover the van

Once the box and dishes are familiar, it's your first time going into the parked van – initially without a running engine. Doors and windows are closed or reliably secured with cat-safe nets. The Cats are allowed to sniff calmly and decide for themselves whether they want to use would like to come to their open box.

Familiar scents help: their blankets, a familiar basket, a Scratching board and a toy from home. I'll stay calm, leave the radio and loud noises and end the first visit after a few relaxed minutes. Next time, there might be a small Meal or a game round in the van. Thus, the vehicle should be removed from the other vehicle Step by step, become a familiar place.

Only after that do I start the engine without starting off. Here too, it begins the training with a very short session. Stay Timmy and Susi Calm down, the first mini-round follows another day – maybe just once around the block. After that, we go back home. The routes are only extended if the previous level is really good worked.

My planned setup looks like this:

  1. open transport box in the apartment
  2. short time in the closed box
  3. Visit in a stationary van with the engine off
  4. a few minutes in the stationary van with the engine running
  5. very short ride in the secured box
  6. Drive from ten to twenty minutes
  7. quiet day trip without overnight stay
  8. a rehearsal night near home
  9. First short camping weekend with a fast Return option

This plan is not a calendar. Maybe one stage will work after two Days, maybe we need several weeks. I will be Timmy and Susi individually, because the close connection as sisters means not automatically that both situations are experienced the same way. What kind of Susi easy can already be too much for Timmy – or vice versa.

During the Travel: Safety comes before freedom

As tempting as the image of a cat on the dashboard may seem: During the ride, my cats stay in their secured Transport boxes. A free-roaming cat can stop during emergency braking be seriously injured, get caught under the pedals, or the driver distract.

The stalls must stand firmly and must neither slip nor tip over. Experts often recommend the rear floor of the vehicle as a safe place a front seat. If a box is fastened with the seat belt, it must they are expressly suitable for this type of fastening. I'll check that based on the manufacturer's specifications for my specific box and my Vehicle.

During breaks, a fixed order applies: all doors and windows first then open the box. I never open at a rest stop at the same time the vehicle door and the transport box. A single A moment of shock might be enough to make a cat in an unfamiliar environment runs away.

A small home on Wheels

In a camper, cats need not just one spot, but several clearly separated areas. Even in a small space, I'm planning:

  • one protected resting and retreat place per cat
  • an elevated viewpoint at the secured window
  • a small scratch surface
  • separate feeding and water bowls
  • easily accessible litter boxes
  • clear paths so that no cat blocks the other in one corner can

Loose items are stored before each trip. cleaning agents, Medications, cords, trash, food, and plants toxic to cats are out of reach. Windows and roof hatches may only be opened if their fuse is truly cat-proof. A normal Fly screens don't necessarily withstand a determined cat.

Temperature is especially important. A camper can quickly Heating up time dangerously. Shadow, an open window, or a Fans are no guarantee that the interior will remain safe. At warm weather, I therefore do not leave the cats alone in the parked Vehicle. I plan trips to cooler times of day whenever possible, control the temperature and always provide fresh water.

litter box In the camper: clean, familiar, and accessible

The litter box is not a side issue, but a crucial one Feel-good factor. Cats often react sensitively to a new Litter type, a different type of toilet, or an unfavorable location. That's why I first take exactly the bedding they bring from home to know.

For multi-cat households, the general recommendation is: a Toilet per cat plus an additional one, distributed among various Places. In the van, that is spatially difficult. For the first tests, I would like to therefore find out whether two compact but sufficiently large toilets can be accommodated sensibly. They should not directly stand side by side, be reachable at all times, and far enough from food and keep water away. If there isn't enough space, solve I'm not just blaming the cats, but discussing the suitable design with a cat-experienced veterinary practice or Behavioral counseling.

A spreading shovel must also be brought along, sufficient Replacement litter, sealable waste bags, a straw mat, and a Enzymatic cleanser for minor mishaps. I clean the Toilets at least in the morning and evening, as well as immediately as needed. If a cat suddenly stops urinating, strains heavily, often without success goes to the toilet or blood is visible in the urine, this is not Travel problem to wait and see, but a veterinary emergency.

Food and water: change as little as possible

On the first trip, I don't want to have the food at the same time switch. The usual food comes portioned and airtight packed with. For a few extra days, I'll take a backup with me in case we are on the road for an unplanned longer period or the well-known variety at the holiday destination is not available.

Each cat gets its own bowl. I offer water at several quiet spots and never directly next to the litter box. Wet food can additionally provide liquid, provided the cats are used to. For longer trips, I plan safe breaks in the closed vehicle, in which I can provide water and toilet. Before the first tour, I also discuss with the veterinary practice how I adjust feeding times sensibly to the trip – especially when when a cat tends to feel nauseous.

The sisters also have different approaches to coat care Needs. I will regularly brush Timmy's longer coat on the go and after small burrs, dirt, or beginning matting search. Susi's short coat is easier to care for, yet it also belongs for she brought a soft brush in her luggage. After an outing, I check on both paws, fur and skin on ticks or small Injuries.

My packing list for two cats in the camper

To ensure nothing is missing before departure, a fixed cat crate is placed in the Van:

  • two sturdy, suitably sized transport boxes
  • two familiar blankets and separate sleeping areas
  • Escape-proof harnesses and lightweight leashes
  • EU pet passports, chip numbers, and vet contacts
  • familiar food plus reserve
  • several sturdy bowls and sufficient drinking water
  • Cat litter boxes, well-known litter, shovel, and litter mat
  • Feces bags, paper towels, and enzymatic cleaner
  • scratching mat and a few familiar toys
  • Brush, tick remover, and veterinarian-recommended parasite control
  • individual medications and a small travel pharmacy to veterinary consultation
  • Towels, spare blankets, and washable pads
  • current photos and my contact details in every box

A GPS tracker can later be an additional aid but replaces neither the microchip nor the registration. He must surely be in the right place A harness must be secured and must not obstruct the cat.

From what I can tell that it becomes too much

Not every meow immediately means panic. Nevertheless, I would like to Observe body language carefully. Warning signs can include strong panting, persistent drooling, vomiting, diarrhea, frantic attempts to escape, permanently attached ears, very large pupils, trembling, freezing, or unusual aggression. Even if a cat has been exposed for a long time neither eat nor drink or use the toilet, I take that Seriously.

Then it's not: "She has to get through now." I break the exercise go back to the last relaxed level and let go of strong or Clarify recurring complaints and health causes. The Well-being the cats are more important than any planned route.

The first The campsite is deliberately unspectacular

For the first night, I'm not looking for a noisy large square with Entertainment, lots of dogs, and constant coming and going. Better is a Quiet, shaded spot near the home. This way I can Stop at any time if a cat can't settle down.

Before booking, I clarify whether cats are allowed and what rules apply. apply. On site, I watch out for free-roaming dogs, traffic, toxic Plants, open windows, and every possible gap in the van. When loading and A fixed "lock rule" applies when exiting: A door is only open when Timmy and Susi are safely in their stalls or at the dishes are under control.

The first outing doesn't have to be spectacular. When both eat, drinking, using their toilet, sleeping, and curiously out the window Look, that's already a great success for me.

My Conclusion: Maybe our next adventure will begin quietly paws

Camping with cats is not a project for me that can be done on a weekend is done. It's a slow getting to know each other: How do Timmy and Susi in the box? What does the engine do to them? Find a safe place to sleep? Can they also be in unfamiliar environments Relax?

At about one year old, Timmy and Susi are young, curious, and Capable of learning – but despite their shared backgrounds, they are two different personalities. That's exactly why I train small steps, with familiar things, rewards, and many Retreat options. There is no fixed date on which they "finish" "Camping cats" must be.

Maybe someday we'll sit together by a quiet lake, while two curious noses watched the world behind the secured window observe. Perhaps our first great journey will come much later take place. And maybe one of them will show you that the home is preferable. Then that will also be accepted.

Because true Vanlife doesn't mean for me every plan for every To carry out the prize. It means freedom – and that applies to mine as well Cats.
