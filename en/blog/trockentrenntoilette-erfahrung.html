8 months on the road with the dry separation toilet: My honest experience

July 2026 · 6 min. reading time · from your Tussy

When I first dealt with the topic of camper toilets before my big trip with the T4, it quickly became clear to me: a chemical toilet was out of the question for me. The dry separation toilet immediately convinced me in terms of concept. Today, after eight months on the road with exactly this system on board, I can say: I am absolutely thrilled – even if not everything went perfectly right away.

The bumpy start with my BOXIO

My first dry separation toilet was a BOXING. The compact format and the idea of separation sounded just right for my little T4 at first. However, I was not satisfied with the specific product in everyday life. I had several suggestions for improvement and for a while seriously considered building my own dry separation toilet based on the basic principle.

The biggest problem: The outlet or funnel through which urine flows into the container has wobbled. If you sat on top, the connection wasn't stable enough – and sometimes the urine went off. The result: it didn't end up in the container, but in the box itself. That was anything but pleasant and had to be thoroughly cleaned every time afterwards. That's exactly the point you want to avoid with a good dry separation toilet.

The trade fair in Düsseldorf and the perfect solution

The turning point came when I happened to be at a trade fair in Düsseldorf and discovered another company there that offers a dry separation toilet where exactly these weaknesses were fixed. All the problems that bothered me with my first model simply didn't exist here. The urine drain was firm and stable, nothing wobble, nothing went wrong. I will gladly link this company to you as soon as I provide the details – because I can truly recommend this solution without reservation.

Why urine separation is so brilliant

What still excites me most about the whole system is something very simple: you can simply dispose of urine in any regular toilet. Whether at a rest stop, in a café, or at friends' – you simply empty the canister into the toilet bowl, flush, and you're done. No hazardous waste, no special disposal station, no chemicals. This is a huge relief in everyday life, especially when, like me, you spend a lot of time in places where there are no designated motorhome disposal stations.

My solution for the solids sector

For the fixed site – affectionately called "the code" in camper jargon – I created my own little system during the trip, which worked really well. I got myself pellet cat litter and brought it with me. Additionally, I had small organic waste bags with me. My approach: First, put a garbage bag in the second container, then a handful of this pellet cat litter so that the moisture is reliably absorbed.

The result really surprised me: it never stinked. At most, it smelled slightly of sawdust, of wood – a truly pleasant, neutral scent. And the best part: I didn't have to painstakingly clean the container. That's exactly the point, I think, that most people are disgusted by when they think about a dry separation toilet – the idea of having to clean the solid waste container by hand. I totally understand that, of course it's not nice. But with my bag solution, that step was completely omitted.

An unexpected advantage: the waste problem

What I hadn't even considered at first: This method also solved a completely different problem that kept occupying me while traveling – waste disposal. When you travel a lot, you eventually notice that many public trash bins only have very small entry slots. You often can't get large trash bags in there, and generally, as a camper, I try to produce as little waste as possible and then dispose of it sensibly. With the small organic waste bags from my toilet system, I automatically always had small, well-sealable portions that could be easily disposed of anywhere. An ideal side solution for a problem I previously had to solve separately.

Space-saving and suitable for everyday use

Another plus I wouldn't have underestimated: the size. My dry separation toilet was small enough to disappear into a shelf down in my T4 in its own box. Everyday life was incredibly simple: pull out the box, get the job done, put the lid on, slide the box back – done. No effort, no hassle, and above all: no odors inside, even though the system was stored so compactly.

My conclusion after eight months

In the end, I wasn't satisfied with my BOXIO – there were too many small but annoying design flaws for my everyday life. I still find the principle of the dry separation toilet itself sensible. With a more stable solution that matches my setup, it saves space and is straightforward; with my cat litter and organic bag solution, cleaning could also be minimized.

For those still wondering whether switching from chemical toilets is worthwhile: From my point of view, definitely yes. Only when choosing the model is it worth looking closely and not grabbing the first quote – I would have liked to skip the second research from the start.
