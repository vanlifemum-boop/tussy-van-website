Expansion & DIY

The T4 VR6 as an everyday camper — space wonders, DIY hacks, and maximum flexibility

How it all began · approx. 3 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Hello, this is Justine from Tussy Van! 💛 Let's start right at the beginning — with the bus that started everything for me.

When you think of life in a camper, you often immediately picture the classic picture: a rigidly installed bed, a fixed kitchenette, and space for exactly two people. But for me, my T4 VR6 had to be a true all-rounder – a vehicle that manages the balancing act between spontaneous road trips and the hectic everyday life with friends. The solution? An absolutely pragmatic space concept and a series of clever DIY hacks that transformed the bus into a self-sufficient home on wheels in no time.

The room concept: Sleeping sideways for maximum flexibility

Probably the biggest highlight of my outfit was the use of the space behind the rear seats and the last three-seater. Unlike the classic Multivan concept, where you often have to fold down the entire seat and lay out the bed completely lengthwise, we chose a different option: we slept sideways in the back. Since the bed did not block the entire interior, the three-seater remained fully operational. That was ideal! Even when we were fully packed, we could still easily bring friends along. Leaving at six in the T4 and making spontaneous trips was no problem. There was enough space for everyone – everyday practicality par excellence.

Water, gas, and the sanitation revolution

Later, airline rails were added to the roof for even more transport options, but the core of the interior design was self-sufficient supply. I needed fresh water in the car. Instead of expensive special tanks, I got a simple 20-liter glass canister. With a matching electric pump attachment, it was quickly transformed into a mobile water station. So I always had boiling water, drinking water, and—if needed—clean water ready for washing. The gallons were stacked space-savingly, and right underneath the gas cylinder found its safe spot, which I could conveniently open and close as needed.

There was also a steep development curve regarding sanitation. At first, it was incredibly simple: a simple toilet bucket, a plastic bag, and a handful of cat litter. It worked, was flexibly replaceable, and easy to dispose of. However, the real revolution came with the installation of a custom dry separation toilet in the lower compartment. Suddenly, the camper setup was complete – clean, odorless, and perfectly integrated.

Power and gadgets without solar panels

Since I didn't yet own a solar panel at that time (which only came much later with my next buses), a different energy solution was needed. I got myself a large mobile starter kit. With it, I could not only start the car myself in an emergency but also easily charge my USB devices and tablets via the built-in ports. During the ride, the package was simply recharged via the cigarette lighter. Additionally, I had a small inverter (converter) for the cigarette lighter to draw normal household power for other devices when needed. Most of the time, it was enough to recharge, but the good feeling of being prepared for anything always came along. With this setup, supplemented by running boards and a tow rope in the lower compartment, I basically always had everything you need for the next adventure with me.
