Piaski–Granica — at the end of the road before Kaliningrad

Polish Baltic Sea · approx. 6 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Some travel destinations can be recognized by a famous building. At Piaski–Granica, it was a large information board in the middle of the forest. At the top was "Piaski–Granica," next to it the R10. On the map, the line continued eastward — to the state border and the Russian region of Kaliningrad.

A narrow strip of land between two bodies of water

Piaski lies on the Polish Vistula Spit. On one side is the Baltic Sea, on the other the Vistula Lagoon. The official tourism website of the Pomeranian Voivodeship describes the R10 feeder road from Jantar via Krynica Morska to the state border at Piaski. From the ferry in Mikoszewo to the border is just under 60 kilometers on the route described there.

I wasn't there to cross a border. My goal was this special point at the end of the accessible path. The photo shows the board, the forest, and some snow on the ground. It feels calm — and at the same time, you can feel that the next seaside resort doesn't just begin behind this place.

Why this place occupied me

A national border is just a line on a map. On site, it feels different. The road ends, signs become more important, and political reality suddenly comes very close to a beautiful coastal landscape. It was precisely this contrast that stayed in my mind: nature, cycling route, and holiday region on one side; Strictly guarded external border on the other.

🧭 Important for a visit: Stay only on open paths, observe closures and notices on site, and inform yourself about the current situation before traveling. An information board at the border area is not a border crossing.

The R10 as a quiet path there

The regional tourism site describes the last section behind Krynica Morska as a forest stretch with gravel up to the border. This exact environment can also be seen in my photo. Those who come by bike will find Pomorskie.Travel the official route description and GPX information.

Piaski–Granica was not a place for a long list of sights for me. It was a quiet, somewhat surreal moment of travel. I stood in front of a map, saw the line to Kaliningrad, and knew: My route does not continue here. Sometimes it is precisely this end of a road that remains the image of a journey.
