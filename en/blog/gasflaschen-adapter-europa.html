Gas cylinder adapters in Europe: Which connection fits in which country?

September 2026 · 11 min. reading time · from your Tussy

You're parked with the camper somewhere in Europe, the German gas bottle is empty – and the bottle in the store looks familiar, but its valve is not at all. Euro adapters are available precisely for this moment. But D1, D2, D3, and D4 are not four magic universal plugs. The key is whether you want to connect a foreign cylinder to your system or have a German cylinder filled by an authorized filling station.

⚠️ Important in advance This overview is a travel aid, not an assembly manual. Bottle valves and suppliers can change even within a country. Always compare the labeling on the valve with the manual of your adapter set. Work only without pressure, do not use damaged seals, and inspect every newly made connection with a suitable leak detector. In times of uncertainty, the work belongs in the hands of a gas specialist company.

First, clarify: removal set or filling set?

In retail, the names sound almost the same, but technically they are two different things. A Outlet or connection adapter connects a gas cylinder purchased or rented in the destination country with a German pressure regulator. The gas flows from the foreign cylinder into your camper facility. A Fill adapter on the other hand, it serves to connect a German gas cylinder to the filling facility of an authorized European filling station.

The designations D1 to D4 are used for both tasks. However, the matching internal and external threads are designed as counterparts. Therefore, it is not enough to simply order "D4". The packaging must also clearly state Removal/Connection or Filling stand. A filler nozzle is not a sampling nozzle – even if both look similar.

The well-known fuel adapters are something else ACME, Dish, Bayonet, and Euronozzle. They belong to permanently installed gas tanks or approved, refillable tank gas cylinders. Cartridges according to EN 417 also form their own system. The four D-adapters described here do not replace tank adapters, pressure regulators, high-pressure hoses, or gas filters.

Is your travel destination missing? That doesn't mean there's no gas there. It only means that no reliable general recommendation can be derived from the set of four. Ask your local bottle supplier about connection, regulator pressure, exchange system, and permitted use in the vehicle.

Why country lists can never be one hundred percent complete

Gas cylinders are not determined by a country alone. Large and small bottles, propane and butane, regional brands, exchange systems, as well as older and newer valves can have different connections. Italy, Switzerland, Slovenia, and the United Kingdom are good examples: several D-adapters appear in manufacturer lists there. Anyone who simply screws on the first adapter from the table risks a crooked thread or a leaky connection.

The safe order is therefore: first inspect the valve, then compare the embossing or thread specification with the set instructions, and finally check whether the part is really suitable for Extraction or Filling is thought. Adapters must never be forcibly "made" to fit. Even additional sealing tapes, unsuitable O-rings, or self-built intermediate pieces have no place in a liquefied gas system.

How to proceed safely when changing bottles

  1. Turn off all gas appliances, keep ignition sources away, and close the bottle valve.
  2. Work only in a well-ventilated bottle crate and make the connection completely pressure-free.
  3. Check valve, adapter, and seal for dirt, cracks, deformation, and missing parts.
  4. Install the explicitly fitting outlet adapter according to the manufacturer's instructions. Do not forcibly turn the left-hand thread in the wrong direction.
  5. Correctly connect the pressure regulator or the designated high-pressure hose. The adapter itself does not regulate pressure.
  6. Slowly open the valve and check all newly connected areas with an approved leak detector.
  7. If blisters form or you smell gas: close the valve immediately, ventilate, and do not use the system. Do not use a flame for leak detection.

Have your German gas cylinder filled abroad

A Euro filling set is not a free pass for self-service at an autogas pump. Conventional German exchange bottles may only be filled by an authorized filling station and according to the applicable local regulations. The filling station decides whether to accept your bottle. Condition, labeling, inspection period, and permissible fill quantity must be correct. After filling, the nozzle is removed again; it does not remain as a permanent connection to the bottle.

If a filling station refuses, a local loan or exchange bottle with the right dispensing adapter is often the better solution. For longer trips, a properly installed, approved tank gas cylinder can also be useful. However, for that, you need the appropriate tank adapters and an installation designed for this purpose—not the D1 to D4 filling kit from this post.

My sensible gas travel box

  • the route-appropriate, clearly labeled sampling set,
  • the original manufacturer's manual, both digital and on paper,
  • intact replacement seals exactly for this set,
  • approved leak detection spray,
  • work gloves and the tools provided by the manufacturer,
  • Photos of your own regulator, hose, and cylinder markings,
  • Address of a gas company or camping dealer at the nearest destination.
The short mnemonic D1 to D4 help you with European bottle connections. However, they do not automatically say whether you are currently holding a filling or removal adapter. Select the country, check the valve, read the product manual, install without pressure, and then always check the tightness.

Conclusion: The table is the start, not the last test

With the right Euro set, you can travel much more relaxed on many European routes. In particular, D4 covers many common systems, while D1, D2, and D3 become crucial for certain countries and providers. Nevertheless, the focus on the specific bottle is more important than any national flag. It's better to take five minutes for threading, sealing, and instructions – gas is a wonderful travel companion, but not an area for improvisation.
