My first camper toilet — simple, small, and anything but glamorous

Camper Everyday Life · approx. 5 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Hardly anyone likes to talk about camper toilets — until you can't find a toilet anywhere at night. For me, it was clear early on: Even in the smallest T4, I need my own solution. Not luxurious, not permanently installed, but always reachable.

My first solution was a simple bucket toilet

In the photos, you can see my first camper toilet: a compact container with a seat and lid. Inside, a bag was inserted, along with absorbent material. After use, everything could be locked and the toilet put back in its place. It required no water connection, no chemicals, and no fixed installation.

For my setup back then, that was exactly the advantage. I was able to take them out, rearrange, and clean them. When every centimeter really counts on the bus, a mobile solution is sometimes more honest and practical than an elaborate restroom you don't even have.

What worked well in everyday life

  • It was small enough for my available storage space.
  • I was independent at night and at remote pitches.
  • There was no liquid that could swirl around during the ride.
  • With a fresh bag, she was quickly ready to use again.

And what bothered me about it

Of course, this first solution was not perfect. Everything ends up in a container, so you have to work very cleanly and dispose of it regularly. Even when storing it, I don't want open or poorly sealed bags in the living room. Hygiene, hand washing, and a fixed routine are absolutely part of it for me.

Later, I tried other systems. My first dry separation toilet was a BOXING — and I was not satisfied with this specific model. I still find the principle of separation useful, but that doesn't mean every product automatically fits into my everyday life. So my simple bucket toilet and the BOXIO are two different milestones in my toilet story.

🚽 My honest conclusion: The best camper toilet is not the most expensive, but the one that reliably fits into your own space, daily routine, and disposal routine. My first solution was simple — but it gave me exactly the independence I needed at the time.
