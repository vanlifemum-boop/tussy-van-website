My camper packing list — what do I really have with me?

Packing list from practice · approx. 8 min. reading time · from your Tussy

"What do you actually have with you?" I keep getting this question. The honest answer: quite a lot — but not randomly. After months in the T4, almost every item had a task and a fixed place.

Sleep and warmth

  • Mattress, bedding, and extra warm blankets
  • Pillows and something to keep moisture under the mattress in check
  • Warm sleepwear for cold nights
  • for winter tours: working heating and suitable fuel

Cooking and supplies

  • Cooking area and my small oven
  • Pot, pan, baking pan, and coffee maker
  • Knives, boards, cutlery, cups, and a few sturdy plates
  • Spices, shelf-stable basic ingredients, and water
  • Dishwashing liquid, cloths, and small garbage bags

Hygiene and toilet

  • Mobile toilet solution with matching bags and suction material
  • Toilet paper, hand sanitizer, and laundry
  • Towels that dry quickly
  • a closed, easily accessible storage unit

Power, tools, and roadside assistance

  • Charging cable for phone and camera
  • Auxiliary battery and the associated connections
  • Tools for small tasks, fuses, and lighting sources
  • Safety vest, warning triangle, and first aid kit
  • Vehicle documents, protection letter data, and important phone numbers

Living Outdoors

  • Small camping table and seating options
  • Outdoor rug for the space in front of the sliding door
  • Roof or luggage bag for light, bulky items
  • Flashlight, extension, and adapters suitable for space
📦 My most important packing rule: Don't just ask: "Can I use this?" But: "How often will I really use it — and where will it stay during the ride?" Everything loose is secured, heavy things come down.

What you don't have to include

Duplicate kitchen appliances, too many clothes, and large supplies for every conceivable situation take up space. In Europe, I can buy or wash almost everywhere. This exact realization has made my packing list smaller and more honest with every trip.

My list is not a rigid law. Summer, winter, weekends, or ten months change the demand. But the categories remain: sleeping, eating, staying clean, having electricity, driving safely, and living outdoors. When these areas work, even a fully packed T4 doesn't feel like chaos.

See you soon and always have a good trip,
your Tussy 💛
