10 months living in a very small space — what Vanlife really did to me

10 Months Vanlife · approx. 7 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Ten months in a T4 sounds like boundless freedom to some, and to others like claustrophobia. For me, they were both — and much more. I slept by lakes, cooked in the rain, reorganized a thousand times, and learned how little space a home actually needs.

The most beautiful room was often outside

The photo from my bed overlooking the lake shows the side that immediately connects you with Vanlife. Door or window open, water in front of your nose, no hotel room could replace this view. On mornings like these, the small interior feels huge because the landscape simply belongs to it.

But outside, it's not always postcard weather. In rain, wind, or cold, life suddenly takes place on the bus. Sleeping, dressing, cooking, working, tidying up — all in just a few square meters and often on the same space. Then you quickly realize whether the expansion really fits your everyday life.

Every item must earn its place

In an apartment, something can lie unused in a drawer for months. In the T4, it's annoying every day. Little by little, I sorted things out, reorganized things, and created permanent spots. What was often needed had to be accessible. What would only "maybe someday" be useful was allowed to work.

Order became a daily movement

Vanlife doesn't mean packing perfectly once. It means constantly switching between driving and living. Move bedding, clear the cooking area, take out the table, put the table in, dry shoes, store cables. Over time, these maneuvers became automatic. Nevertheless, it remained work.

💛 My most honest learning: Less space doesn't automatically make life easier. It just shows faster what works, what bothers you, and what really matters to you.

What I have won

I became more patient, inventive, and independent. I've learned to solve problems with whatever is right there. At the same time, I understood how important retreat, warmth, and a functioning toilet can be. Freedom is not about needing nothing — but about knowing your own needs.

Ten months in the smallest space didn't make me the perfect minimalist. But you showed me that home is not a floor plan. At home, it can be a red T4 in the woods, a bed by the lake, or simply the place where my things have their place and I can close the door behind me.

See you soon and always have a good trip,
