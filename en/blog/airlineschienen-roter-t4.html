Expansion & DIY

New airplanes for the red one — finally space for luggage on the roof

Expansion on the Red · approx. 5 min. reading time · from your Tussy

In a T4, every free centimeter eventually becomes valuable. At first, you still think: This all fits in. Then come the table, carpet, tools, supplies, and all the things that make life on the go pleasant. With the Red, it quickly became clear: If bulky luggage was to disappear from the interior, the roof had to cooperate.

Why I chose Airlineschienen

I didn't want a rigid solution that would only fit a single box. Airline skis give me multiple attachment points and thus more freedom when packing. Depending on the trip, a roof or luggage bag can be attached at the top, while the interior remains free for living. Especially on longer tours, this difference is huge: I don't have to rearrange half my home every evening before I can go to bed.

In the photo, you can see the two rails running lengthwise on the roof. That's exactly how I wanted it: simple, sturdy, and without unnecessary frills. However, the rails themselves are only part of the solution. The decisive factor is always the entire system consisting of roof, fastenings, suitable fittings, lashing devices, and the actual roof load of the vehicle.

What is allowed on my roof

My principle is simple: bulky and as light as possible upwards, heavy downwards. This not only improves order but also keeps the focus reasonable. Anything that could get wet or is sensitive also needs weatherproof packaging. Before each departure, I check whether the straps and attachment points are secure. After the first few kilometers, I check again — better once too much than once too little.

⚠️ Important for roof luggage: Permissible roof load, total weight, vehicle height, and the clearances of all parts used must match. Drilling and sealing are not for "we'll be fine." If you're unsure, have the installation professionally checked.

More space means more peace

The biggest gain for me was not the roof itself, but the quieter interior. Things suddenly have a fixed place. The seating and sleeping areas remain usable, and you don't have to move an avalanche of luggage when stopping. It's exactly such small modifications that gradually turn a car into a home.

That's why the airline skis weren't just a decoration project, but a practical decision for my real travel routine. They didn't make the red one bigger—but they made the available space feel much bigger.
