More storage space in the trunk — my simple shelf in the red T4

Expansion on the Red · approx. 5 min. reading time · from your Tussy

The trunk of a T4 looks big — until bedding, tools, supplies, table, and camping gear are inside. Without structure, everything is stacked on top of each other. Of course, you always need exactly the box that's at the very bottom. My solution was surprisingly simple: an extra layer in the rear.

Height becomes storage space

In the first photo, you can still see the new wooden panel almost empty. It divides the trunk into two sections. At the top, there is room for larger and lighter items; at the bottom, boxes, tools, and equipment can be kept. That way, I use not only the ground but finally also the height.

The second photo shows reality after clearing. It is not a glossy finish and not a perfectly organized exhibition. It's a real travel suitcase. But things are no longer all in one heap, and I get to what I need much faster.

My rules for packing in the back

  • Heavy objects as deep as possible and as close as possible to a stable boundary.
  • Frequently needed items remain accessible without completely clearing out.
  • Light, bulky items are allowed on the upper level.
  • Loose parts are secured for the ride.
  • Emergency equipment must never disappear behind half the house.
🔧 Not a piece of furniture, but a system: The record alone does not create order. Only fixed seats and a sensible order create truly usable storage space from additional volume.

Why this small renovation has changed so much

When traveling, you're constantly clearing out: sleeping, cooking, driving, shopping, sleeping again. Every unnecessary search costs time and nerves. With the second level, I was able to switch from driving mode to living mode more quickly. And that's exactly what matters in everyday life more than any particularly fancy detail.

My shelf was simple, but it matched my red and my way of traveling. For me, van conversion doesn't mean installing as much as possible. It means solving your own problems in a way that makes life easier on the road.
