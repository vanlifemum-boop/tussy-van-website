Algarve in Winter — Travel Tips for My Big Travel Wish

Updated September 2026 · 8 min. reading time · from your Tussy

💛 Frankly: I haven't been to the Algarve before. Therefore, this article is not a travel report and does not contain personal experiences. He collects my travel wishes and the information I would check for a future camper trip.

The Algarve has long been on my wish list: Atlantic coast, golden cliffs, small towns, and a winter break from the gloomy weather. But anticipation is not the same as experience. That's why I'm deliberately writing here about what excites me about the region, what a possible trip might look like, and what I would prepare for before departure.

Why the Algarve is on my wish list

The official Portuguese tourism portal describes the Algarve as a region with a mild climate, beaches, cliffs, islands, and a mountainous hinterland. It's exactly this mix that makes them interesting to me: not just standing by the sea, but also hiking, getting to know places, and slowly traveling from East to West.

Still, I wouldn't plan a winter trip as a guarantee for summer weather. In the Atlantic, wind, rain, and cool nights can be part of it. For me, the appeal would be precisely the quieter travel time — with enough time and without the expectation of having to sit on the beach every day.

This is what my desired route could look like

I wouldn't check off the Algarve in just a few days, but rather plan several different regions:

  • Tavira and the Ria Formosa: In the east, I want to get to know the lagoon landscape, islands, salt pans, and birdlife. According to Visit Portugal, the Ria Formosa stretches over about 60 kilometers and can be explored on marked trails as well as partly by boat.
  • Faro and Olhão: Here, I would connect the city, markets, and coast and take time for everyday life away from the mere beach scenery.
  • Silves: The city in the hinterland is on my list because of its history and traces of the Arab past.
  • Lagos and Ponta da Piedade: I want to experience the distinctive rock formations on foot from safe viewpoints; a boat tour would only be possible under suitable weather and sea conditions.
  • Sagres and Cabo de São Vicente: The rugged southwest with a wide Atlantic view would be a goal for me — but not a free pass to spend the night on a cliff.
  • Costa Vicentina: For this protected coastal section, I would plan especially carefully, stay on marked paths, and use only permitted overnight spots.

That's a list of ideas, not a route I've already taken. I would check opening hours, access points, and seasonal offers again shortly before the trip.

Camping with a camper: Check the rules first

The most important planning point for me is not the most beautiful view, but a legal spot for the night. According to the current information from Visit Portugal for traveling by motorhome overnight stays in Natura 2000 areas, protected areas, and coastal management plan areas are generally not permitted — except at explicitly permitted locations.

In other areas, according to this official overview, motorhomes may stay a maximum of 48 hours in the same municipality, unless a different municipal rule applies. Designated campsites and motorhome service areas have their own terms and conditions. Signage and local regulations may change and must always be observed on site.

My rule for the later trip: Do not automatically consider coastal parking spaces as sleeping areas. I would mark campsites or explicitly allowed motorhome sites in advance and additionally plan for a legal alternative option.

Costs: better to plan than to invent numbers

As long as I haven't been there myself, I deliberately don't mention any supposedly "real" total costs. The budget depends too heavily on travel, vehicle, length of stay, choice of pitch, and personal daily life. For my planning, I would calculate these items separately:

  • Fuel, tolls, and possible environmental zones on arrival and return
  • Campsites or official motorhome pitches
  • Food, restaurant visits, and local markets
  • Gas, heating, shore power, water, and laundry
  • Admissions, boat tours, and other activities
  • a real reserve for repairs and unplanned overnight stays

Only after my own trip can I seriously write down here what I really spent.

Gas supply: clarify before departure

I also don't want to pretend to have experience with gas that I haven't had. Which connection, regulator, or bottle replacement fits depends on the built-in system. Before the trip, I would check the supply for Portugal, procure suitable and approved parts, and ask a specialist company if uncertain. Improvised modifications to the gas system are out of the question for me.

You can find a general overview in my article Gas cylinder adapters in Europe. On site, I would still confirm technical specifications with the provider.

What belongs in the camper in winter

Mild does not automatically mean warm and dry. For a winter trip to the Atlantic, I would therefore pack warm clothing, rain protection, good blankets, and a plan against moisture. Ventilating, heating, and drying wet items must work even if there are several days without postcard weather. I would also check in advance which campsites, laundromats, and supply points are actually open during the off-season.

What I wish for from this trip

I don't want to collect as many photo spots as possible. My wish would be to travel slowly: walk by the water in the morning, visit small towns and markets, hike inland, shop regionally, and treat the delicate coast with respect. If a spot is full or closed, for me it's also about continuing to drive.

💛 My honest conclusion: The Algarve is a travel wish for me, not yet an experience. I can't say today how three months there feel or what they cost. If I really made the trip, I will supplement this post with my own photos, real issues, places visited, and my personal conclusion.
