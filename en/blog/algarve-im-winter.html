The Algarve in winter — planning my dream camper trip

Updated September 2026 · 8 min. reading time · from your Tussy

💛 Full transparency: I have not been to the Algarve yet. This article is therefore not a travel report and does not claim first-hand experience. It brings together my travel wishes and the information I would check before a future camper trip.

The Algarve has been on my wish list for a long time: an Atlantic coastline, golden cliffs, small towns and the thought of escaping grey winter weather for a while. But anticipation is not the same as experience. So this is an honest look at what draws me to the region, how a possible trip could take shape and what I would prepare before setting off.

Why the Algarve is on my wish list

Portugal's official tourism portal describes the Algarve as a region of mild weather, beaches, cliffs, islands and a mountainous interior. That combination is exactly what appeals to me: I would not only want to stay by the sea, but also walk, discover towns and travel slowly from east to west.

I would not treat a winter trip as a guarantee of summer weather. Wind, rain and cool nights can be part of life beside the Atlantic. For me, the quieter season is part of the attraction — provided I allow enough time and do not expect a beach day every day.

What my wish-list route could look like

I would not try to tick off the Algarve in a few days. I would make room for several very different areas:

  • Tavira and Ria Formosa: In the east, I would like to discover the lagoon, islands, salt pans and birdlife. Visit Portugal describes Ria Formosa as stretching for roughly 60 kilometres, with designated walking opportunities and some areas accessible by boat.
  • Faro and Olhão: I would combine the coast with town life and markets, making time for the everyday Algarve beyond the beach scenery.
  • Silves: This inland town is on my list for its history and traces of the region's Arab past.
  • Lagos and Ponta da Piedade: I would like to see the distinctive rock formations from safe viewpoints; I would consider a boat trip only in suitable weather and sea conditions.
  • Sagres and Cabo de São Vicente: The rugged south-west and its wide Atlantic views appeal to me — but a dramatic cliff-top car park is not automatically a legal place to sleep.
  • Costa Vicentina: I would plan especially carefully in this protected coastal area, stay on marked paths and use only authorised overnight locations.

This is a collection of ideas, not a route I have already driven. I would check opening times, access and seasonal services again shortly before travelling.

Sleeping in a camper: check the rules first

For me, the most important planning point is not the prettiest view but a legal place for the night. According to the current guidance on Visit Portugal's official campervanning page, overnight stays in Natura 2000 areas, protected areas and zones covered by coastal management plans are generally prohibited unless a place is expressly authorised.

Elsewhere, when no different municipal rule applies, the official guidance allows motorhomes to stay overnight for no more than 48 hours within the same municipality. Designated campsites and motorhome service areas have their own conditions. Local signs and rules can change and must always be followed.

My rule for a future trip: I would never assume that a coastal car park is an overnight site. I would mark campsites or expressly authorised motorhome areas in advance and keep a legal backup option for every stop.

Costs: plan them instead of inventing them

Until I have actually travelled there, I will not publish supposedly “real” totals. A budget depends too much on the route, vehicle, length of stay, choice of overnight places and daily habits. I would calculate these categories separately:

  • fuel, tolls and any environmental zones on the outward and return journeys
  • campsites or official motorhome areas
  • groceries, meals out and local markets
  • gas, heating, hook-up electricity, water and laundry
  • admission fees, boat trips and other activities
  • a genuine reserve for repairs and unplanned overnight stays

Only after my own trip could I responsibly say what I really spent.

Gas supply: work it out before departure

I do not want to pretend I have tested a setup in Portugal when I have not. The correct connector, regulator and bottle arrangement depend on the system installed in the vehicle. Before travelling, I would check the Portuguese supply options, obtain approved parts and ask a qualified professional whenever I was unsure. Improvised alterations to the gas system would not be an option.

My general overview is available in Gas cylinder adapters in Europe. I would still confirm technical details with the supplier before using any part.

What I would pack for winter

Mild does not automatically mean warm and dry. For a winter trip beside the Atlantic, I would pack warm layers, rainwear, good blankets and a practical plan for moisture. Ventilation, heating and drying wet clothes still need to work when the weather is not postcard-perfect for several days. I would also confirm which campsites, laundries and service points are actually open in the low season.

What I hope to find on this trip

I do not want to race between photo spots. My wish is to travel slowly: walk beside the water in the morning, visit small towns and markets, hike inland, shop locally and treat the fragile coastline with respect. If a place is full or closed, moving on is simply part of responsible travel.

💛 My honest conclusion: The Algarve is a travel wish for me, not an experience yet. I cannot honestly say what three months there feel like or what they cost. If I make this journey, I will update this article with my own photographs, actual spending, places I visited and a genuine personal conclusion.
