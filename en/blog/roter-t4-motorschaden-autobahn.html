The Red One Has Left Us — Engine Failure on the Highway

Workshop Story · approx. 5 min. reading time · from your Tussy

There are breakdowns after which you replace a part, take a deep breath, and keep driving. And there is that one moment when you feel: This is bigger. At the red one, that moment came on the highway. Engine damage. End of the ride.

From now on, everything comes to a halt

On the highway, safety comes first: get out of the danger zone, hazard lights, keep distance from traffic, and organize assistance. There is really no room for big emotions at this moment. They come later — when the car is parked, the hood is open, and you realize that a small repair won't be enough.

I'm deliberately not writing down any fabricated diagnoses or supposed warning signs here. The only certainty was: the engine was broken and the red one wouldn't carry me further. That's exactly why I prefer to show him as he truly belonged to my life: on the road, inhabited, and full of memories.

It wasn't just a car

The red was the kitchen, bedroom, storage room, and retreat. I had built on it, invented solutions, made mistakes, and lived in it for months. That's why you don't just lose a vehicle with such an engine failure. You lose the spaces, routines, and plans you have associated with it.

Every scratch had a story. The airplanes, the shelf in the rear, the gas system, the heating, my little stove — none of that was just any equipment. It was my self-assembled home. That's exactly why the farewell hurt so much.

💔 What remains of the red: The trips, the modifications, and everything I learned from him don't disappear with an engine failure. This knowledge was later incorporated into the blue.

The end of a stage, not my journey

On the day of the engine failure, everything felt final. Today I see: The Red was a whole stage of my vanlife story. It ended hard and much earlier than I wanted. But she also showed me how much I could do by now and how much this life belonged to me.

The red one has left us. Tussy Van has remained. And eventually, the Blue came — with new possibilities, new construction sites, and, as you will still read, its very own workshop drama.

See you soon and always have a good trip,
