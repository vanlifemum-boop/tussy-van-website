Between splendor, concrete and the sound of the sea: My honest look at Gdansk & the Tricity

Poland · approx. 7 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Hello, this is Justine from the Tussy Van! 💛 When you think of Gdansk, you usually immediately think of the postcard motifs: the beautifully restored, colorful patrician houses of the old town, the famous crane gate and the maritime flair on the Motława. And yes — the old town is absolutely gorgeous! When you stroll through the streets, you can hardly escape the historical charm.

But if you take a closer look and don't just check off the city in quick succession, you won't just see the polished postcard, but also the reality off the beaten track.

The Gdansk split: beautiful old town vs. gray concrete

My honest conclusion: I am still ambivalent about Danzig to this day. As soon as you leave the historic old town, you quickly end up in a completely different world. The rest of the city is often just a "rough block": gray, utilitarian, not very exciting and, to be honest, not particularly worth seeing. Sure, there are huge shopping malls like everywhere else - but let's be honest: a mall is a mall.

❄️ My insider tip for the winter: There is one exception for which Gdansk is worthwhile even outside the old town season - the Christmas market! It has this very special, almost magical flair. You should have experienced this once in your life: atmospheric, glittering and absolutely worth seeing for everyone.

My personal highlight: Sopot and Gdynia

But for me, the most beautiful thing about the region is not at all Gdansk alone, but the combination of the entire "Tricity" (Trójmiasto). Because the real feeling of life is just a few train stops away - in Sopot and Gdynia.

Sopot: Directly to the dream beach in two stations

Sopot was my absolute dream place. From Gdansk it's just a few minutes away. two stops by train - and you are in a completely different world. The brilliant thing: The train station is super central, you can only walk from there two to five minutes and it's perfect for clearing your head, walking for hours and breathing in the fresh Baltic Sea air Spa resort vibe that immediately puts you in vacation mode.

Gdynia: Maritime heartbeat and modernity

Just a little further - about four to five train stations away from Gdansk - is Gdynia. While Sopot is the charming Baltic Sea resort, Gdynia scores with its very own, modern and maritime energy. Here, too, you can be on the water in a flash, enjoy the harbor atmosphere and soak up modern Polish coastal life to the fullest.

The "Gold of the Baltic Sea": Why the Amber Museum is a must

There is one topic that you simply cannot avoid in Gdansk: Amber. The fossil resin is omnipresent here, and it is not for nothing that the city is considered the world capital of amber. If you want to understand the true fascination behind the "Gold of the Baltic Sea", you have to go to Amber Museum (Muzeum Bursztynu). Honest opinion? Museums can sometimes be dry - but this one captivated me from the first second.

The museum has found a new home that is worth the visit alone: the Big Mill (Wielki Młyn), a huge medieval brick mill from the 14th century. The contrast couldn't be better - raw, historical charm with thick walls on the outside, and an ultra-modern, stylish and perfectly lit exhibition on the inside. The amber pieces are presented like diamonds in a luxury boutique: elegant, clean and high quality.

My personal highlights of the exhibition:

  • The inclusions — small prehistoric time machines: many pieces contain perfectly preserved insects, small lizards or plant parts, millions of years old. Through magnifying glasses you can see details as if the little animals had gotten there just yesterday. Natural history you can touch.
  • The Amber Room of Modernity: real works of art far beyond the chains of yesteryear - dresses completely covered in amber, huge ones Ship models, guitars and avant-garde jewelry that would also work on the catwalks of Paris or Milan.
  • The gigantic giant amber: one of the largest pieces of amber in the world - a colossal chunk that weighs several kilos.
💡 My insider tip for the visit: Take it easy 1.5 to 2 hours Time. The exhibition is interactive, so you can immerse yourself completely without reading the text for a long time. And then meant by the famous Frauengasse (Ulica Mariacka) stroll - the most beautiful alley in Gdansk, packed with small amber studios. You look at the displays with completely different eyes and suddenly recognize the difference between cheap tourist kitsch and real, handcrafted art.

The best museum in Europe: The Museum of the Second World War

I have already seen many museums, cultural sites and historical places in Europe - but what awaited me in Gdansk can hardly be put into words. Quite directly and without exaggeration: The Museum of the Second World War (Muzeum II Wojny Światowej) is the best, best, best museum I've ever been to in Europe. The way the story is presented here is not just informative - it is an emotional journey that leaves you deeply touched and speechless. If you come to Gdansk, must here.

Even from the outside, the building is an eye-catcher: a modern, spectacular tower that protrudes crookedly from the ground like a wedge into the future, while the actual main exhibition is underground - an architecture that perfectly symbolizes the turning point that the war meant. As soon as you enter the underground halls, you notice: a whole new level has been reached here.

  • No dry year battle: You walk through reconstructed pre-war streets, which turn into deserts of rubble during the course of the exhibition.
  • The focus on people: It's not just about military strategies, but above all about the fate of the civilian population - worldwide.
  • Multimedia and gripping: Light, sound, original exhibits and interactive elements give you goosebumps non-stop. You're not just a viewer - you literally feel the story.

My personal insider tip in the same complex: the scientific Museum library with a huge, multilingual book collection. The heart of it is the lady who works there - she once built the library from scratch and provides advice with infectious passion Important: A visit to the library is only with advance notice possible.

📍 Good to know for your visit:
  • Address: Muzeum II Wojny Światowej, pl. Władysława Bartoszewskiego 1, 80-862 Gdańsk
  • Museum library (pre-registration mandatory): biblioteka@muzeum1939.pl · +48 58 760 09 60
  • Web: muzeum1939.pl

Conclusion: The mix do it

Gdansk is a great starting point for exploring the Polish Baltic Sea coast. Even if the city itself has its rough edges and shows a lot of gray concrete away from the old town, the proximity to the sea, the fast connections to Sopot and Gdynia and the glamorous amber side make the region something very special. And to be honest: No matter how conflicted you are when you look at the contrasts - the Amber Museum captures the sparkling side perfectly, and the Museum of the Second World War shows how powerful and touching good history communication can be.

By the way: Gdansk was also the northernmost point of my big 6-week road trips from Cologne across Europe — followed the call of the north to the Baltic Sea. 🌊
