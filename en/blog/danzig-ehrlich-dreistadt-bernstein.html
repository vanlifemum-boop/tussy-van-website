Between splendor, concrete, and the sound of the sea: My honest view of Danzig & the Tricity

Poland · approx. 7 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Hello, this is Justine from Tussy Van! 💛 When you think of Gdańsk, you usually immediately think of postcard motifs: the beautifully restored, colorful patrician houses of the old town, the famous Krantor, and the maritime flair on the Mottlau. And yes — the old town is absolutely stunning! Strolling through the alleys, it's hard to escape the historic charm.

But if you look more closely and don't just check out the city in a fast forward, you see not only the polished postcard but also the reality off the tourist path.

The Gdańsk Split: Beautiful Old Town vs. Gray Concrete

My honest conclusion: I am still divided about Gdańsk to this day. As soon as you leave the historic old town, you quickly find yourself in a completely different world. The rest of the city is often just a "rough block": gray, functional, not very exciting, and honestly not particularly worth seeing. Sure, as everywhere there are huge shopping malls — but let's be honest: a mall is a mall.

❄️ My insider tip for winter: There is one exception for which Gdansk is also worthwhile outside the Old Town season — the Christmas market! It has this very special, almost magical flair. You should experience that at least once in your life: atmospheric, sparkling, and absolutely worth seeing for everyone.

My personal highlight: Sopot and Gdynia

But for me, the most beautiful thing about the region is not just Gdańsk alone, but the combination of the whole "Tricity" (Trójmiasto). Because the true feeling of life is just a few train stops away — in Sopot and Gdynia.

Sopot: In two stops directly to the dream beach

Sopot was my absolute place of longing. From Gdańsk, it is just two stops by train — and suddenly you find yourself in a completely different world. The genius: The train station is super central, from there you can only walk two to five minutes and stands on the wide sandy beach. Perfect for clearing your head, walking for hours, and breathing in the fresh Baltic Sea air. Sopot has that light, elegant spa vibe that instantly makes you feel like you're on vacation.

Gdynia: Maritime Heartbeat and Modernity

Just a little further — about four to five train stations from Gdańsk — lies Gdynia. While Sopot is the charming Baltic Sea resort, Gdynia scores with its own unique, modern, and maritime energy. Here too, you're at the water in no time, can enjoy the harbor charm, and soak up modern Polish coastal life to the fullest.

The "Gold of the Baltic Sea": Why the Amber Museum is a Must

There's one topic you simply can't ignore in Gdańsk: amber. The fossil resin is ubiquitous here, and the city is not called the world capital of amber for nothing. Anyone who wants to understand the true fascination behind the "gold of the Baltic Sea" must Amber Museum (Muzeum Bursztynu). Honest opinion? Museums can sometimes be dry — but this one captivated me from the very first second.

The museum has found a new home that alone is worth a visit: the Great Mill (Wielki Młyn), a huge medieval brick mill from the 14th century. The contrast couldn't be better — raw historical charm with thick walls on the outside, and a state-of-the-art, stylish, and perfectly lit exhibition inside. The amber pieces are presented like diamonds in a luxury boutique: elegant, clean, and high-quality.

My personal highlights of the exhibition:

  • The inclusions — Small primeval time machines: Many pieces contain perfectly preserved insects, small lizards, or plant parts, millions of years old. Through a magnifying glass, you can see details as if the little creatures had just gotten in yesterday. Natural history up close.
  • The Amber Space of Modernity: true works of art far beyond the chains of the past — dresses completely adorned with amber, huge ship models, guitars, and avant-garde jewelry that would also work on the runways of Paris or Milan.
  • The gigantic giant amber: one of the largest pieces of amber in the world — a colossal chunk weighing several kilos.
💡 My insider tip for the visit: Take it easy 1.5 to 2 hours Time. The exhibition is interactive, so you can fully immerse yourself even without reading long texts. And then through the famous Frauengasse (Ulica Mariacka) stroll — the most beautiful street in Gdańsk, packed with small amber studios. You look at the displays with completely different eyes and suddenly see the difference between cheap tourist kitsch and genuine, artisanal art.

The best museum in Europe: The Museum of World War II

I have already seen many museums, cultural sites, and historical sites in Europe — but what awaited me in Gdańsk is hard to put into words. Directly and without exaggeration: The World War II Museum (Muzeum II Wojny Światowej) is the best, best, best museum I have ever been to across Europe. How the story is unfolded here is not simply informative — it is an emotional journey that deeply moves you and leaves you speechless. Whoever comes to Gdańsk, must here.

Even from the outside, the building is a striking sight: a modern, spectacular tower that juts crooked out of the ground like a wedge into the future, while the main exhibition lies underground — an architecture that perfectly symbolizes the turning point the war signed. As soon as you enter the underground halls, you realize: a whole new level has been reached here.

  • No Dry Years Battle: You walk through reconstructed streets from the pre-war era, which transform into deserts of rubble over the course of the exhibition.
  • The focus on people: It is not just about military strategies, but above all about the fate of the civilian population — and that worldwide.
  • Multimedia and engaging: Light, sound, original exhibits, and interactive elements keep giving you goosebumps. You are not just a viewer — you almost feel the story.

My personal insider tip in the same area: the scientific Museum Library with a huge, multilingual book collection. Her heart is the lady who works there — she once built the library from scratch and advises with infectious passion. Important: A visit to the library is only with prior appointment possible.

📍 Good to know for your visit:
  • Address: Museum II Wojny Światowej, pl. Władysława Bartoszewskiego 1, 80-862 Gdańsk
  • Museum Library (Advance registration required): biblioteka@muzeum1939.pl · +48 58 760 09 60
  • Web: muzeum1939.pl

Conclusion: The mix makes the difference

Gdańsk is a great starting point for exploring the Polish Baltic Sea coast. Even though the city itself has its nooks and crannies and shows a lot of gray concrete away from the old town — the proximity to the sea, the fast connections to Sopot and Gdynia, and the glamorous amber side make the region something truly special. And honestly: no matter how conflicted you are when looking at the contrasts — the Amber Museum perfectly captures the sparkling side, and the World War II Museum shows how powerful and moving good historical communication can be.

By the way: Gdańsk was also the northernmost point of my large 6-week road trips from Cologne across Europe — followed the call of the North all the way to the Baltic Sea. 🌊
