The heart of Poland: Warsaw's hidden treasures, a golden goose and magical museum nights

Poland · approx. 7 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Hello, this is Justine from Tussy Van! 💛 Warsaw (Warszawa) is a city that fascinated me from the first second. It is loud, modern, full of contrasts and carries an incredible story. When you walk through the streets, you can feel at every corner that this city has risen like a phoenix from the ashes. In addition to the moving history, there are also wonderful legends and very special events that you simply have to experience. Here are my absolute highlights and insider tips!

The Legend of the Golden Goose

If you're traveling in Warsaw's Old Town, sooner or later you'll come across the history of the Golden Goose. But where exactly is she supposed to hide? The legend leads directly into the underworld of the city - into the cellars of the historic Ostrogski Palace, which now houses the famous Fryderyk Chopin Museum.

🪿 The legend of the Golden Goose: Hundreds of years ago, it was said that in the deep, flooded vaults of the Ostrogsky Palace lived a beautiful golden goose - not a normal animal, but an enchanted princess who guarded an untold treasure. One day a poor shoemaker named Lutek ventured down and actually found the underground lake and the goose. She said: "I'll give you 100 gold ducats - but you have to spend it all on yourself in a single day. If you share even a penny, you lose everything." Lutek bought the finest clothes, ate in the most expensive restaurants - and still had coins left in the evening. Then he met a hungry old war veteran who asked him for alms. His heart was too good: he broke the rule and gave the remaining coins to the beggar. A huge bang - the goose disappeared forever, everything bought vanished into thin air. But Lutek wasn't sad: he understood that you can't buy happiness and was much happier because of his honest work.

If you visit the Chopin Museum today in the ulica Okólnik 1 visit, think of the cellar vaults and the golden goose that might still be waiting there!

The Palace of Culture: Stalin's "gift" to the city

A building that you simply cannot miss in Warsaw is the monumental Palace of Culture and Science (Pałac Kultury i Nauki). At over 230 meters high, it is still one of the most striking landmarks today. The huge building in the style of socialist classicism was a "gift" from Josef Stalin to the Polish people in the 1950s - for many a long time a symbol of oppression, today accepted by the younger generation as a cult location.

Inside there are cinemas, theaters and museums. But the absolute highlight is the Viewpoint on the 30th floor (Trzydzieste Piętro): a fantastic 360-degree view of the entire metropolis. The interplay of modern skyscrapers and historical scenery is simply phenomenal.

The Night of the Museums: magical hours in the castle

Warsaw is a paradise for culture fans - and the Night of Museums (Noc Muzeów). Especially the Royal Castle (Zamek Królewski) opens its magnificent gates this night. Walking through the golden halls at a late hour is an indescribable feeling, and the terraced museum garden sloping down to the Vistula is magically illuminated.

The event traditionally takes place in Poland once a year instead (not twice, as some suspect) - always in the middle May, usually on the third Saturday. Then almost all museums, galleries and even political institutions are open until late at night, and entry is completely free. The atmosphere in the whole city is electric.

The university library: a secret roof garden

Another place for the list is the Library of the University of Warsaw (BUW). The building made of glass and green steel is already an architectural masterpiece - but the real jewel is upstairs: one of the largest and most beautiful Roof gardens of Europe, divided into two levels, connected by streams, bridges and sculptural plants. During the day you can walk here wonderfully with a view of the Vistula. And on Museum Night there is often the unique opportunity to climb to the roof at the latest hour - seeing the illuminated city from this green paradise is unforgettable.

Conclusion

Warsaw is full of life and full of history. Whether you're following in the footsteps of the Golden Goose, enjoying the view from Stalin's controversial Palace of Culture or wandering through the museums at night in May - this city is always worth it. Pack your bags with great anticipation, because Warsaw will surprise you!
