Morąg: a museum stop on the way to Masuria

November 21, 2026 · Poland · approx. 5 min. reading time · from your Tussy

Between lakes and forest, a bit of art can also be found on my Poland route. The Museum of Upper Prussia in the Dohna Palace in Morąg is an exciting stopover for anyone heading towards Masuria.

The German-language museum brochure gave me this travel idea: portraits from Dutch studios, sculptures, objects from old manor houses and stories from the surrounding area, all under one roof. That is why I am giving the museum a post of its own. If you enjoy discovering places between the major holiday destinations, add Morąg to your list.

Where is Morąg located – and why does it fit the Masurian trip?

Morąg, known in German as Mohrungen, is in today’s Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship. Historically, the town belongs to the Oberland, or Upper Prussia, west of Masuria proper. In my section Discover Masuria I suggest the town as a possible stop on the way to the lakes.

A palace that tells history itself

The Museum of Upper Prussia (Muzeum Prus Górnych) is housed in the Dohna Palace. The building’s history spans its original houses and connecting tower, a fire and Baroque reconstruction, wartime destruction and rebuilding. Following a further renovation, the museum reopened in March 2025. The palace itself provides an introduction to the region’s history.

Seven exhibitions to discover

The brochure presents these seven permanent exhibitions; they are also included in the current museum program. I would pick out the topics that make me particularly curious and give them a bit more time.

1. Dutch Portraits from the Dohna Collection

The portraits from the 17th century depict members of the Dohna family and Dutch society. Many paintings used to hang in the residences of Schlodien and Reichertswalde. Names like Gerrit and Willem van Honthorst or Pieter Nason represent the artists in this collection. My suggestion: Look at clothing, posture, and small objects—what did the people portrayed want to say about themselves?

2. Memorabilia from Prussian Manor Houses

Furniture, art, and objects from former living spaces offer insights into the world of regional manor houses. The exhibition connects these pieces with the families and houses to which they belonged. This way, things that might otherwise only appear decorative regain their history.

3. Sculptures by Adolfo Wildt

The Italian sculptor Adolfo Wildt is presented with works from the collection of his patron Franz Rose. They were part of the furnishings of the estate in Döhlau, today's Dylewo. His early works show a different facet of the artist than his later better-known pieces. For art enthusiasts, that would be a reason for me to take a closer look.

4. Burial Mounds and the Cultural Landscape of Upper Prussia

This exhibition goes much further back. Burial mounds tell of burial forms and the people who shaped the landscape long before today's cities. The brochure describes individual sites and entire groups that are often preserved in forest areas. A great opportunity to see the landscape with new eyes as you continue your journey.

5. Morąg and its surroundings

Maps, old views, photographs, and documents show how the city has changed. The marketplace, town hall, streets, and train station become traces of everyday life. The Dohna family and Johann Gottfried Herder, born in Mohrungen, also belong to this history. I would visit this room before taking my own city walk.

6. The History of Dohna Palace

Plans, historical photographs, and digital representations explain the development of the building and its use. The reconstruction in the late 1970s, the museum opening in 1986, and the recent renovation connect the older history with the place you can visit today.

7. Lutheran Epitaphs and Memorial Portraits

Here, art, memory, and religious history meet. Such memorial works often combine painting, sculpture, and architectural forms. An example is the epitaph for Peter and Catherine of Dohna, which originally comes from the church in Morąg. The exhibition shows how religious ideas were expressed in the memory of the deceased.

This is how I would plan the stopover

For a first tour, I would reserve about one and a half to two hours – as a personal planning idea, not as a fixed visit duration to the museum. Afterwards, a walk towards the marketplace and town hall is recommended. Afterwards, there is time for a break before continuing on your own route. Especially on a rainy day, such a cultural stop can be a nice addition to a trip to the lakes.

With children, I would choose two or three topics and do small search tasks from them: Which portrait stands out first? What details do you recognize in the old cityscapes? For arrival with the camper, I would separately check a suitable parking spot; Overnight accommodation is not included with this museum tip.

Address, opening hours, and admission

Museum of Upper Prussia / Muzeum Prus Górnych
Dohna Palace, ul. Dąbrowskiego 54
14-300 Morąg, Poland

  • October to May: Tuesday to Sunday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m.
  • June to September: Tuesday to Sunday, 10 a.m.–5 p.m.
  • Closed on Mondays. The ticket office closes half an hour earlier each time.
  • Admission: regular 28 zł, reduced 16 zł.
  • Tuesdays: free admission to the permanent exhibitions; Special exhibitions are excluded from this.

Information checked on 21 November 2026. Before travelling, especially on public holidays, check the current opening hours and ticket prices on the museum website.

My travel tip for your Poland route

Those heading towards Masuria who want to experience not only water and nature but also art and regional history will find a suitable idea for their own stop in Morąg. What appeals to me especially is the mix: first getting to know the people and houses of the region, then walking through the city outside.
