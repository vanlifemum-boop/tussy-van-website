Old gas system removed from the trunk — converted into a spare wheel space

Gas conversion at the Roten · approx. 6 min. reading time · from your Tussy

The red one could run on gasoline and gas. Practical for refueling — less practical was the space occupied by the old gas system in the trunk. A large part of the valuable rear was permanently blocked as a result. For a camper I wanted to live and travel in, that was not a good solution in the long run.

The plan: Gas tank down

Under the vehicle was the space for the spare wheel. That is exactly where the gas plant was supposed to move. The goal was clear: to regain storage space at the top of the trunk without giving up the engine's gas operation. In the photos, you can see both sides of the story — the tank in the rear and later the work on the floor or at the new installation location.

Such a conversion is much more than just "unscrewing the tank and fixing it somewhere else." Fastening, wiring, protection against damage, distance to heat sources, ground clearance, corrosion, tightness, and the required tests must be compatible. It must also be clarified what happens to the spare wheel when its space is reused.

⚠️ This is not a replica manual: Autogas systems belong in expert hands and must be inspected and accepted according to applicable regulations. The photos show my conversion and my experience — no approval for another vehicle.

What the free trunk has brought

After the renovation, the difference was immediately noticeable. Where previously technology dominated the space, luggage and equipment could be stored more sensibly. Together with the extra storage level, the rear finally became what I needed: a well-usable storage space instead of a compromise around the tank.

Every renovation has a downside

More space at the top means responsibility at the bottom. The new installation location must be considered at every inspection. The absence of a spare wheel or alternative accommodation is also part of your travel planning. That's exactly what I learned during expansion: There is rarely a solution without consequences. Good solutions are those whose consequences are known and accepted.

For my Red, the conversion was still an important step. The technology remained part of the vehicle, but it no longer controlled my entire trunk. And in a small camper, reclaimed space is almost as valuable as an extra room.
