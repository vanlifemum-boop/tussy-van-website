Campsite, parking space or free standing? The big overnight guide for campers

Guide · 7 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

Where are we sleeping tonight? This question accompanies every camper trip - and the answer shapes costs, comfort and experience. Both worlds have their place. Here's the honest comparison.

The campsite: comfort with neighbors

The Advantages: Electricity, fresh water, waste disposal, warm showers, often WiFi, bread service and playground – all in one place. Plus security through reception and fence as well as quick contact with other campers. The campsite is unbeatable for families with children and for "service days" (laundry, tanks, extensive shower).

The disadvantages: Costs from 25 to over 60 euros per night in high season, narrow pitches, noise from neighbors, fixed arrival and departure times - and full places in summer, which require a reservation. Spontaneity looks different.

Standing free: Freedom with responsibility

The Advantages: Free, lonely dream places, absolute peace, sunrise without a neighboring caravan in the picture - that's the camper life from dreams. Plus maximum flexibility: arrive when you want, drive when you want.

The disadvantages: No infrastructure - water, electricity and toilet have to come from the vehicle. The legal situation is completely different depending on the country: from permitted to tolerated to strictly forbidden with high fines. And it takes experience to find good places and assess them safely.

How to find good free spaces

Parking space apps with user reviews are the standard - current comments reveal whether a space still exists and what it really looks like there. The satellite view of the map services also helps. If possible before dark an: You see the surroundings, recognize prohibition signs and don't seem suspicious.

Stand safely and respectfully

  • Trust your gut feeling: If a place seems strange, keep driving. There is always someone else.
  • Don't spend the night within sight of problem areas or directly at lonely motorway parking lots that are known for break-ins.
  • Stand so that you can move forward at any time and have the keys ready.
  • Stay in the same place for a maximum of one night, don't put any furniture out and behave inconspicuously - overnight stays yes, camping no.
  • Leave the place cleaner than you found it. Every garbage bag that is left lying around creates the next prohibition sign.

The golden mix

Most experienced campers combine: two or three nights free or on cheap pitches, then a campsite to "refuel" - laundry, water, disposal, long shower. This keeps the budget slim and comfort maintained.

Conclusion

There is no better or worse - only the right one for the day. Campsites provide comfort and security, freestanding provides freedom and silence. If you respect the rules of the country, choose places with common sense and leave no trace, you get the best of both worlds.

The question of all questions on every camper trip is: Where are we tonight? The answer determines costs, comfort, safety and, last but not least, the feeling of traveling. Between the five-star campsite with a pool area and the lonely bay without a soul there is a whole spectrum of possibilities. This guide shows the advantages and disadvantages of all options - and how you can find the most beautiful places safely and legally.

Option 1: The campsite

The classic with full infrastructure: electricity on the plot, Sanitary buildings with warm showers, fresh water, disposal station, often WiFi, washing machines, bread service, restaurant and playground.

Strengths: You don't have to worry about anything at the campsite. Children immediately find playmates, the sanitary facilities save work in the on-board bathroom, and after a few days on the road, all the tanks can be filled or emptied and laundry can be washed here. The feeling of security is also high: reception, lighting, other campers all around. For families and beginners, the campsite is the most relaxed start.

Weaknesses: The comfort costs - in the high season in popular destinations it can cost 40 to 60 euros and more per night. The pitches are often narrow, the neighbor hears every word, and during the holidays good spots are hard to come by without a reservation. Fixed check-in and check-out times and lunchtime rest with the barrier closed limit flexibility. If you're looking for loneliness, you won't find it here.

Option 2: The parking space

Between the campsite and the freestanding area is the motorhome parking space: a designated area, often on the outskirts, at the harbor or at the farm, usually with basic services (supply and disposal, sometimes electricity) for little money - often 5 to 20 euros per night, sometimes free.

Parking spaces are the backbone of many trips: cheap, uncomplicated, without barriers or registration. Concepts in which winemakers, farms or restaurants leave campers overnight for free in the friendly expectation that you will try their products are particularly charming. This is often how the most beautiful encounters of the trip arise.

Option 3: Freestanding - the dream of the lonely bay

Being free means spending the night outside designated places - on the edge of the forest, on the mountain pass, with a view of the sea. It's the image that most people have in their minds about camper life.

The sunny side: free, quiet, lonely and often breathtakingly beautiful. No neighbor, no barrier, no schedule. Wake up, open the door, nature.

The dark side: zero infrastructure. Water, electricity, toilet – your vehicle has to supply everything, which requires self-sufficiency in terms of battery, solar and tanks. And above all: the legal situation differs massively from country to country. Some countries generously allow you to spend the night in a vehicle, others allow a night to "restore driving ability", others strictly prohibit it and impose severe fines - especially in coastal regions and national parks. Find out about the current rules before every border and respect local prohibition signs without exception.

How to find good free spaces

The most important tools are parking space apps with community ratings: millions of registered locations, current comments and photos reveal whether a space exists, is legal and quiet. In addition, the satellite view of the map services is worthwhile to assess the surface and access - a rain-soaked dirt road quickly becomes a trap for a 3.5-ton vehicle.

Practical basic rules:

  • Arrive in daylight. You recognize prohibition signs, neighborhood and underground - and do not appear suspicious to residents.
  • Have plan B. Always save two or three alternatives nearby in case the place you want is occupied, blocked or unsuitable.
  • Don't park in crowds: Lonely motorway service areas are statistically the worst choice - that's where most break-ins happen. Better a quiet village than the anonymous parking lot on the transit route.

Safety when standing freely

The good news: Serious incidents are rare. The basic rules make them even rarer:

  • Take your gut feeling seriously. If a place seems strange - groups standing around, broken glass, broken machines - move on. There is always another place.
  • Park ready to escape: Nose towards the exit, nothing in front of the vehicle, keys ready to hand on the bed at night.
  • Valuables invisible Stow away, lock the driver's cab, if necessary additional security on the doors.
  • Send the location to someone – a short message with coordinates to family or friends costs nothing and is reassuring.

The code of conduct: So that free standing remains possible

Every new prohibition sign in a beautiful place has a backstory - usually garbage, noise or campers who have turned an overnight stay into a base camp. Therefore:

  • Stay overnight, don't camp: no chairs, no grill, no awning outside. The vehicle remains closed and inconspicuous.
  • One night, then further - whoever stands in the same place for days is provoked Complaints.
  • No traces: Take rubbish with you (including strangers), never throw wastewater into nature, and only dispose of toilets at official stations.
  • Be quiet - no music, no running generator next to residential buildings.

The golden mix

Most experienced campers drive a rotation model: two to four free or cheap ones Parking nights, then a campsite stop for laundry, tanks, long showers and a bit of comfort. This keeps the budget within limits, the vehicle supplied - and the trip varied.

Conclusion

Campsite, parking space and free standing are not competitors, but tools for different days. You will find comfort and company on the pitch, freedom and silence in nature, the best compromise is often on the pitch. Anyone who knows the rules of the host country, chooses with common sense and leaves every place better than they found it is welcomed everywhere - and experiences camper life at its best.
