Finding the right camper: The big guide for beginners

Guide · 7 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

Alcove, integrated, semi-integrated, panel van - anyone looking for a camper for the first time will be overwhelmed by terms and offers. The selection can be narrowed down with three simple questions: How many people are traveling with you? When and where do you want to go? And how much budget is available?

The most important vehicle types at a glance

Alcove: The classic family mobile with the bed above the driver's cab. Lots of sleeping places, lots of storage space, but built high and susceptible to wind. Ideal for families with children.

Fully integrated: The luxury class. The driver's cab and living space form a unit, huge panoramic window, best feeling of space - but also the highest price.

Semi-integrated: The popular compromise. Original driver's cab, attached living structure, often with fold-down bed. Good ratio of comfort, weight and price – perfect for couples.

Cabin van (campervan): Compact, maneuverable, suitable for everyday use. Fits into normal parking spaces and is ideal for spontaneous trips and free standing. But less space and storage space.

Renting: the best way to get started

Before you buy, rent. One or two rental trips will show you which size and layout really suits you - and whether camper life suits you at all. The rental costs are money well invested: a bad purchase costs many times over. Book early in the high season, check everything thoroughly when handing over and document any damage with photos.

New or buy used?

A new vehicle offers a guarantee, optional equipment and modern technology - but it loses a lot of value in the first few years. A used one is significantly cheaper, but you bear the risk of hidden defects.

There is an iron rule when buying used: Moisture is the biggest enemy. Damp damage to the body can ruin the vehicle economically. Therefore, check all corners, window frames, roof hatches and the floor - ideally with a moisture meter or together with an expert. Also pay attention to complete maintenance records, the condition of the gas system and tires (age counts, not just tread!) and always take a long test drive.

Plan a buffer in your budget: registration, small repairs and accessories are almost always included in the purchase price and the basic equipment.

Expand yourself - is it worth it?

Constructing a van yourself is tempting: an individual concept, potentially cheaper, plus the pride in your own work. But don't underestimate the effort. Hundreds of hours of work, specialist knowledge of electrics and gas, tools, material costs and, at the end, the acceptance - for professionals without any experience in the trade, purchasing is usually the more relaxed choice.

Conclusion

There is no one perfect camper - only the one that suits your life. First, clarify the crew size, travel destinations and budget. test different types through the rental and only then buy. And when it comes to used cars: it's better to have one inspection too much than to have moisture damage too late.

The decision was made: a camper was needed. But then the real challenge begins. Alcove or panel van? New or used? Buy, rent or expand yourself? The market is huge, the price range ranges from 15,000 euros for an old used vehicle to well over 150,000 euros for a new luxury vehicle. This guide brings order to the jungle - so that in the end you find the vehicle that really suits you.

Step 1: Clarify your basic questions

Before you even open an ad, answer three questions:

How many people are traveling with you? Every person needs an approved seat with a belt and a place to sleep. A family of four has completely different requirements than a couple or a solo traveler with a dog.

When and where do you want to travel? If you only go to the Adriatic in summer, you don't need winter suitability. If you want to be out and about in the cold season, you should pay attention to insulated tanks, a powerful heater and a heated waste water tank. And those who like to be free will benefit from large water, battery and gas reserves as well as solar on the roof.

What is your budget - honestly calculated? The purchase price includes insurance, taxes, maintenance, parking space, accessories and reserves for repairs. A tried and tested rule of thumb: After purchasing, plan a decent buffer for the initial equipment, from drive-on wedges to cable drums to camping furniture.

Step 2: Learn the vehicle types know

Alcove - recognizable by the "nose" above the driver's cab, in which a double bed is hidden. Advantages: lots of fixed sleeping places, generous storage space, often cheaper than comparable integrated ones. Higher consumption. The classic for families.

Fully integrated - here the driver's cab and living space form a continuous unit with a huge windshield. Advantages: best feeling of space, excellent insulation, driver's seats can be turned completely into the living space. But that's exactly what it's all about, right?

Partially integrated - the standard driver's cab remains, with the living structure behind it. With the optional fold-down bed above the seating area, four people can sleep. Advantages: good price-performance ratio, moderate weight, large selection of models. The bestseller for couples.

Cabin van (campervan) – a converted transporter, usually six meters long and less than three meters high. Advantages: maneuverable, suitable for everyday use, inconspicuous, ideal for free standing and for cities. Disadvantages: less living space, smaller tanks, compact bathroom. Perfect for active couples and solo travelers.

There are also living cabins for pickup trucks, converted minivans and expedition vehicles - niches that are exciting for special needs are.

Step 3: Rent before you buy

The most common beginner mistake is to rush into a purchase. The wiser order: rent first, then decide. One or two rental trips with different types of vehicles will show you mercilessly what you really need - and what only looked good on paper. Maybe you find that the fold-down bed is too narrow for you, that the rear garage is indispensable or that the large alcove is too sluggish on mountain roads.

Practical tips for renting: Book early in the high season, pay attention to fair mileage and deposit regulations, ask for thorough instructions upon handover and document any scratches with photos. The rental costs of a few hundred euros per week are cheap insurance against a five-figure bad purchase.

Step 4: New or used?

New vehicle: Guarantee, desired configuration, latest technology, no hidden previous damage. You pay the full price for this and bear the greatest loss in value over the first few years. Delivery times can also be long.

Used vehicle: Much cheaper, available immediately, the first major loss in value has already been written off. That's why you need care when buying - and this is exactly where it decides whether your camper life begins happily or in the workshop.

Step 5: The visit - this is where the wheat is separated from the chaff

When buying used, one point is more important than all others: Humidity. A leaky body can draw water unnoticed for years, cause wooden structures to rot and economically ruin the vehicle. Therefore:

  • Check all corners, edges, window frames, roof hatches and penetrations - inside and out.
  • Look out for discoloration, bubbles the wall covering, musty smell and soft spots in the floor.
  • Take a moisture meter with you or invest in an expert or a leak test from a specialist retailer.

The checklist also includes: complete service booklet and valid gas test, condition and age the tire (older than six to eight years is a reason to change, even if the tread is good), function of the heater, refrigerator, water pump and on-board electrical system, rust on the chassis and the papers: Is the chassis number correct, are there entries for attachments, how many previous owners?

And always: listen for rattling noises, check the brakes, clutch and directional stability - but time pressure and excuses are warning signs.

Step 6: Self-assembly - dream or trap?

An empty van plus your own ideas: self-construction attracts with individuality and supposed savings. The reality: hundreds of hours of work, costs for insulation, furniture construction, electrics, water and gas installation, plus regulations and approvals that must be adhered to. If you have good craftsmanship, have time and enjoy the project itself, you can create a great, unique piece. If you just want to travel quickly, you'll almost always drive better with a purchased vehicle - and cheaper if you honestly count your own working hours.

Conclusion

The right camper is not just a question of your wallet, but of the fit: crew, travel style, season and budget determine the type. Rent first, buy informed, check used items uncompromisingly for moisture - and don't be blinded by glossy photos. Then the purchase of a vehicle becomes the beginning of a great adventure instead of an expensive lesson.
