The big packing list for the camper trip: The complete guide

Guide · 6 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

The biggest beginner's mistake when packing a camper? Packing too much. Every kilo costs fuel, storage space and nerves - and much of it is never used. Here comes the essence: what you really need to have with you and what you can save.

Safety first

High-visibility vests for all fellow travelers, warning triangles, first-aid kits and fire extinguishers belong in the vehicle - some of them are required by law. Also: spare wheel or breakdown kit, tow rope, jumper cable, flashlight and a small toolbox with cable ties, duct tape and fuses. A CO and gas alarm increases safety when sleeping, as does a smoke alarm.

Kitchen: less is more

Two pots, a pan, a sharp knife, cutting board, crockery and cutlery per person plus a spare set - that's enough. Plus coffee or tea solution to taste, can and bottle opener, lighter, dishwashing set and food storage containers (stackable!). Basic supplies: oil, salt, spices, pasta, rice, canned goods. You buy fresh food on the go - that's part of the experience.

Bath and sleep

Quick-drying microfiber towels, hanging toiletry bags, toilet paper (fast-dissolving for cassette toilets), sanitary additives, biodegradable soap and flip-flops for communal showers. For sleeping: suitable bed linen, a warm extra blanket and, depending on the time of travel, a hot water bottle or fan.

Technology and papers

Charging cable, power bank, adapter, CEE cable drum for shore power, extension cable and a headlamp. Important: driving license, vehicle documents, proof of insurance, foreign health insurance. Identity cards/passports - and copies of them stored separately or digitally secured.

Camping basics

Chocks for balancing, water hose with adapters, camping table and chairs, clothesline with clips, doormat for the entrance and a sun sail or awning make everyday life much more pleasant.

What stays at home

Half the wardrobe ("just in case"), the third pair of shoes, kitchen appliances for special cases, large towels that dry for days, stacks of books (e-readers!), food supplies for three weeks - there are supermarkets along the way. Rule of thumb: If you don't use it at home in two weeks, you won't need it on vacation.

The best trick: the personal list

On your first trip, keep a note with two columns: "was missing" and "never used." After two or three tours you will have your perfect, individual packing list - and in the future you will pack in an hour instead of a weekend.

Conclusion

Packed well is half the journey. Concentrate on safety, slim kitchen equipment, functional textiles and complete paperwork – and leave everything at home that “might” be needed. Your camper, your back and your fuel consumption will thank you.

On their first camper trip, most people pack as if they were going on an expedition to the end of the world. On the fifth trip, everything fits into half of the cupboards - and still nothing is missing. The difference is experience. So that you don't have to pay dearly for it with overloaded cupboards and forgotten essentials, you can find the complete packing guide here: organized by area, with the most important must-haves and the most common unnecessary souvenirs.

Basic rule: Weight is the enemy

A camper has a permissible total weight - and this is reached more quickly than many people think. Water, gas bottles, bicycles and basic equipment already weigh a lot. Every unnecessary kilo increases consumption and wear and tear and, in the worst case, can result in fines during an inspection. Weigh your loaded vehicle on a public scale - the result surprises almost everyone.

Safety and technology: the mandatory equipment

This category includes everything that you hopefully never need, but always have with you:

  • High-visibility vests for everyone (required for every occupant in many countries), warning triangle, first-aid kit
  • Fire extinguisher and fire blanket - ready to hand, not behind three drawers
  • CO alarm, gas alarm and smoke alarm for safe sleep
  • Spare wheel or tire sealing set plus jack that matches the weight of the vehicle
  • Jumper cables, tow rope, work gloves, flashlight/headlamp
  • Basic tools: screwdriver, pliers, open-end wrench, cable ties, duct tape, spare fuses, some wire - this can be used to improvise 90 percent of all small repairs on the go

The documents include: driver's license, vehicle registration document, proof of insurance, green card if applicable, international health insurance, ID cards and passports. Professional tip: Photograph everything once and save it in the cloud, keep copies separate from the original.

Kitchen: the art of reduction

The camper kitchen tempts you to overpack - but cooking on the road is usually easier than at home. What has proven itself is:

  • Two pots (large and small), a good non-stick pan, possibly a kettle
  • One set of crockery and cutlery per person plus a reserve set for guests
  • A really sharp knife, cutting board, spatula, wooden spoon, sieve
  • Can opener, bottle opener, corkscrew, Lighter or matches
  • Coffee solution according to preference - from hand filter to espresso pot
  • Stackable food storage containers (save space and keep opened items fresh), freezer bags, aluminum foil
  • Dishwashing bowl, washing up liquid (biodegradable), Sponges, tea towels

The following applies to basic supplies: oil, salt, Pepper, favorite spices, pasta, rice, a few canned goods and coffee/tea are enough to get you started. You can buy fresh groceries on the way - local markets are one of the best travel experiences anyway.

Bathroom and hygiene

  • Microfiber towels: light, small, dry in hours instead of days
  • Hanging toiletry bag - storage space is in short supply in camping bathrooms
  • Toilet paper (ideally quickly decomposing for cassette toilets) and sanitary liquid
  • biodegradable soap and shampoo on the bottom - important if you shower in nature
  • Flip-flops for communal showers, laundry bags for Dirty laundry
  • Sun cream, insect repellent, personal medication and a small one Travel first aid kit

Sleeping and clothing

For the bedroom: suitable bed linen (camper beds often have special dimensions!), a warm additional blanket and, depending on the season, a hot water bottle or fan.

The onion principle helps when it comes to clothing: a few pieces that can be combined in several layers instead of outfits for every occasion. Functional clothing dries quickly and does not wrinkle. As a guideline, there is enough clothing for about a week - washed on the go. Also: sturdy shoes, comfortable everyday shoes, flip flops, rain jacket. The third pair of “fancy” shoes stays at home.

Electricity, water, Pitch

  • CEE cable drum (at least 25 meters) plus adapter for normal sockets
  • Drinking water hose with various connection adapters and a watering can for refilling on the go
  • Chocks for leveling - sleeping crooked ruins every night, and refrigerators work better when they are level.
  • Camping table, chairs, clothesline with clips, doormat against sand and dirt in the interior
  • Power bank, charging cable, multiple plugs, inverter if necessary

The Zbędnik: What is guaranteed to stay at home

Every experienced camper knows the list of things that go along for a season and are never used: the raclette machine, the stack of books (take an e-reader), the third pair of jeans, the huge terry cloth towel, the "supplies" for three weeks, the entire range of tools for the restoration of a classic car and the decoration for a cozy ambience. Rule of thumb: If you don't use it at home in two weeks, you won't need it on the go. And almost everything can be bought on the go if necessary.

The most valuable tip: your personal list

No foreign packing list is a perfect fit for you. Therefore: On your first trips, keep a simple note with two columns - "was missing" and "never used". When you return, adjust your list. After three tours, you will have a tailor-made packing list that will make packing take an hour instead of a weekend. Many people leave the basic equipment in the vehicle permanently anyway - then driving off just means: clothes, food, papers, done.

Conclusion

The perfect packing list is short, well thought out and personal. Security and documents are mandatory, reduction wins when it comes to kitchen and clothing, and all “maybe baggage” stays at home. This keeps your camper light, your storage space clear - and you have your head free for what it's actually all about: the trip.
