The Big Packing List for Camper Trips: The Complete Guide

Guide · 6 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here is the summary

The biggest beginner's mistake when packing a camper? Packing too much. Every kilo costs fuel, storage space, and nerves – and much of it is never used. Here comes the essence: what really has to be included and what you can save.

Safety first

Warning vests for all passengers, warning triangles, first aid kits, and fire extinguishers are required in the vehicle – some of which are legally required. Included: spare wheel or breakdown kit, tow rope, jump starter cable, flashlight, and a small toolbox with cable ties, duct tape, and fuses. A CO and gas detector increases safety while sleeping, as does a smoke detector.

Kitchen: less is more

Two pots, a pan, a sharp knife, cutting board, dishes and cutlery per person plus a set of spare – that's enough. Along with coffee or tea solution to taste, can and bottle opener, lighter, dish set, and freshness cans (stackable!). Basic supplies: oil, salt, spices, pasta, rice, canned goods. You buy fresh food on the go – that's part of the experience.

Bathing and sleeping

Quick-drying microfiber towels, toiletry bags for hanging, toilet paper (quick-dissolving in cassette toilets), sanitary additive, biodegradable soap, and flip-flops for shared showers. For sleeping: suitable bedding, a warm extra blanket, and depending on the travel time, a hot water bottle or fan.

Technology and Papers

Charging cable, power bank, adapter, CEE cable reel for shore power, extension cable, and a headlamp. Important: driver's license, vehicle papers, proof of insurance, international health insurance, ID cards/passports – and copies thereof stored separately or digitally secured.

Camping basics

Driving wedges for balancing, a water hose with adapters, camping table and chairs, clothesline with clips, a doormat for the entrance, and a sunshade or awning make everyday life much more pleasant.

What stays at home

Half the wardrobe ("just in case"), the third pair of shoes, kitchen appliances for special cases, large towels that dry for days, stacks of books (e-readers!), food supplies for three weeks – there are supermarkets along the way. Rule of thumb: What you don't use at home in two weeks, you won't need on vacation either.

The best trick: the personal list

On your first trip, keep a note with two columns: "missed" and "never used." After two or three tours, you'll have your perfect, individual packing list – and in the future, you'll pack in an hour instead of a weekend.

Conclusion

Well packed is half traveled. Focus on safety, sleek kitchen equipment, functional textiles, and complete paperwork – and leave everything at home that just "might" be needed. Your camper, your back, and your fuel consumption will thank you.

On their first camper trip, most people pack as if they're going on an expedition to the ends of the earth. On the fifth trip, everything fits in half the cupboards – and yet nothing is missing. The difference is called experience. So you don't have to pay dearly with overloaded cabinets and forgotten essentials, here you'll find the complete packing guide: organized by area, with the most important must-haves and the most common unnecessary souvenirs.

Basic rule: Weight is the enemy

A camper has a permissible total weight – and that's reached faster than many think. Water, gas bottles, bicycles, and the basic equipment already weigh quite a bit. Every unnecessary kilo increases consumption and wear and, in the worst case, can cost you fines during an inspection. Weigh your loaded vehicle once on a public scale – the result surprises almost everyone.

Safety and technology: mandatory equipment

This category includes everything you hopefully never need but always have with you:

  • Warning vests for all persons (required for every occupant in many countries), warning triangle, first aid kit
  • Fire extinguisher and fire blanket – within reach, not behind three drawers
  • CO warning detectors, gas detectors, and smoke detectors for safe sleep
  • Spare wheel or tire sealing kit plus jack that matches the vehicle's weight
  • Jump starter cable, tow rope, work gloves, flashlight/headlamp
  • Basic tools: screwdriver, pliers, open-end wrenches, cable ties, duct tape, spare fuses, some wire – these can be used to improvise 90 percent of all minor repairs on the go

Along with the documents: driver's license, vehicle registration, proof of insurance, if applicable green card, international health insurance, IDs, and passports. Pro tip: Photograph everything once and back it up to the cloud, keep copies separate from the original.

Cuisine: the art of reduction

The camper kitchen tempts you to overpack – but cooking on the go is usually easier than at home. The following have proven effective:

  • Two pots (large and small), a well-coated pan, possibly a kettle
  • One set of dishes and cutlery per person plus a set of spare items for guests
  • A really sharp knife, cutting board, spatula, cooking spoon, sieve
  • Can opener, bottle opener, corkscrew, lighter or match
  • Coffee solution to your preference – from hand filters to espresso pots
  • Stackable freshness containers (save space and keep opened items fresh), freezer bags, aluminum foil
  • Dishwashing bowl, dishwashing detergent (biodegradable), sponges, dish towels

For the basic supplies: oil, salt, pepper, favorite spices, pasta, rice, a few canned goods, and coffee/tea are enough to start. You buy fresh food on the go – local markets are one of the most beautiful travel experiences anyway.

Bath and hygiene

  • Microfiber towels: lightweight, small, dry in hours instead of days
  • Toiletry bags for hanging – storage surfaces are scarce in camping pools
  • Toilet paper (ideally fast-decomposing for cassette toilets) and sanitary fluid
  • Biodegradable soap and shampoo – important when you shower in nature
  • Flip-flops for shared showers, laundry bags for dirty laundry
  • Sunscreen, insect repellent, personal medications, and a small travel first aid kit

Sleeping and clothing

For the bedroom: suitable bedding (camper beds often have special sizes!), a warm extra blanket, and depending on the season, a hot water bottle or fan.

When it comes to clothing, the onion principle helps: a few combinable pieces in several layers instead of outfits for every occasion. Functional clothing dries quickly and does not wrinkle. As a guideline, clothing lasts about a week – washing is done on the go. Additionally: sturdy shoes, comfortable everyday shoes, flip-flops, rain jacket. The third pair of "chic" shoes stays at home.

Electricity, water, parking space

  • CEE cable reel (at least 25 meters) plus adapter for standard sockets
  • Drinking water hose with various connection adapters and a watering can for refilling on the go
  • Ramp wedges for leveling – crooked sleeping ruins every night, and refrigerators work better on scales
  • Camping table, chairs, clothesline with clips, doormat against sand and dirt inside
  • Power bank, charging cable, power strip, possibly inverter

The Zbędnik: What is guaranteed to stay at home

Every experienced camper knows the list of things that go on for a season and are never used: the raclette machine, the stack of books (take an e-reader), the third pair of jeans, the huge terry towel, the "supplies" for three weeks, the complete range of tools for restoring a vintage car, and the decorations for a cozy atmosphere. Rule of thumb: What you don't take in your hands at home in two weeks, you don't need on the go either. And almost everything can be bought on the go if necessary.

The most valuable tip: your personal list

No stranger's packing list fits you perfectly. Therefore: On your first trips, keep a simple note with two columns – "was missing" and "never used". After returning, adjust your list. After three tours, you'll have a customized packing list, with packing taking an hour instead of a weekend in the future. Many leave the basic equipment in the vehicle anyway – then all you have to do is set off: clothes, food, papers, done.

Conclusion

The perfect packing list is short, well thought out, and personal. Security and documents are mandatory, the reduction wins when it comes to kitchen and clothing, and all "maybe luggage" consistently stays at home. This keeps your camper light, your storage clear – and you have your mind free for what it's really about: the journey.
