What is Vanlife? The honest look at life on wheels

Guide · 6 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

Vanlife – hardly any other travel trend has inspired so many people in recent years. But what's actually behind it: lifestyle, type of vacation or just a nice hashtag?

The idea behind Vanlife

Essentially, vanlife means living in a vehicle permanently or for a longer period of time - usually in a converted van or camper. It's about more than vacation: living, working and traveling merge into one lifestyle. Less possessions, fewer fixed costs, but more freedom, nature and self-determination - that's the promise.

There are many variations: Some people live in a van all year round, others for months at a time, others spend every free weekend and holiday on wheels. Vanlife is not a competition - every form counts.

Work by on the road

The boom was made possible primarily by working from anywhere. Programmers, designers, copywriters, translators, online consultants or content creators often only need a laptop and stable internet. With LTE/5G routers, additional antennas and solar power, the van becomes a rolling office. Discipline and routines are important: Anyone who works when the sea is sparkling outside needs clear working hours - otherwise either the job or the experience suffers.

The sunny sides

Freedom: Choose your place of residence according to the weather, mood and project. Cost savings: No rent, less consumption - although fuel, maintenance and parking spaces should not be underestimated. Minimalism: Only what is really needed is left on a few square meters. Many see this as liberating. Near nature and new encounters: The vanlife community is open and helpful, you can quickly get into it Conversation.

The dark side

Vanlife is not a permanent vacation. Getting water, disposing of the toilet, doing laundry, looking for a parking space - the logistics of everyday life take time every day. In addition, there is little privacy for two, dependency on the weather, bureaucratic questions (registration address, insurance, postal service) and periods of loneliness when friends and family are far away. The internet is also not reliable everywhere - a real stress factor for working people.

Is Vanlife something for you?

The best test: start small. First spend two or three weeks at a time in the vehicle, work on the road for a few days as a test and honestly observe how you feel about it. Many find that the mixture is ideal for them: a solid base plus several months of the year when traveling.

Conclusion

Vanlife is neither the paradise from the Instagram feed nor the nightmare that skeptics describe. It is a lifestyle with real freedoms and real costs – logistically, financially and socially. If you start with realistic expectations, do small tests and clarify your work situation beforehand, you have a good chance of finding the van not just a vehicle, but a home.

Vanlife: more than vacation, less than utopia

Vanlife refers to living in a vehicle - permanently, seasonally or for longer periods. Unlike a classic camping holiday, living, working and traveling merge into one lifestyle. The basic idea: less possessions, fewer fixed costs, fewer obligations - but more freedom, more nature and more self-determination over your own time.

Important to understand: There is no such thing as one van life. Some people give up their apartment and job and live in a vehicle all year round. Others spend the summer months on the move and overwinter in a fixed location. Still others live normally in an apartment and use every weekend and every vacation for life on wheels. All of this is vanlife - a competition to see who lives more "real" is superfluous.

The vehicles are also diverse: from self-built panel vans to compact campervans and large motorhomes. What's important is not the vehicle, but the attitude: being at home when you're on the move.

Why now? The boom and its reasons

Three developments have turned vanlife from a dropout phenomenon into a mass trend. Firstly, the digitalization of work: more and more jobs can be done from anywhere with a laptop and an internet connection. Secondly, social media, which feeds the longing for freedom and nature with new images every day. And thirdly, a growing discomfort with expensive rents, busy schedules and consumer pressure - the van appears to many as an alternative.

Working on the go: This is how the rolling office works

The basis of most vanlife existences is location-independent work. Typical professions: software development, web design, copywriting and translation, online marketing, virtual assistance, consulting and coaching via video call, photography and content creation related to traveling itself - for example via YouTube, blogs or collaborations. Some also work seasonally on site: harvest helpers, campsite jobs, catering.

Technically, the rolling office needs three things above all:

Internet: A mobile LTE/5G router with a good data tariff is mandatory; an external antenna significantly improves reception in remote regions. Professionals have SIM cards from several providers and check the network coverage before choosing a parking space for working days.

Electricity: Second battery of sufficient size, solar panels on the roof, if necessary an inverter for the laptop - the energy supply must match the workload.

Routines: The underestimated factor. If you go to the beach in the morning, you work in the evening - or not at all. Successful digital nomads structure their days clearly: fixed work blocks, fixed free time. Otherwise either the job or the travel experience suffers, usually both.

The sunny side of vanlife

Radical freedom. The place of residence follows the weather, the projects, the mood. Too hot? Off to the mountains. Too gray? Heading south.

Less costs, less ballast. Without rent and with reduced consumption, you can live more cheaply - although to be honest, fuel, maintenance, insurance and parking spaces have to be taken into account.

Forced Minimalism. Only what is really needed remains in a few square meters. Many describe exactly this as the greatest liberation: fewer things, fewer decisions, more clarity.

Nature and community. Wake up by the lake, finish work by the sea - and on the way an amazingly open community where you can help each other, exchange tips and quickly get into conversation.

The dark sides that are less talked about

Everyday logistics. Filling up fresh water, disposing of wastewater and toilets, washing laundry, cooling food, finding a legal and safe place to park every evening - these tasks cost time and energy every day. Vanlife is not a permanent vacation, but a household on wheels.

Little privacy. If you live as a couple in six square meters, you need a stable relationship and good communication. There are hardly any places to retreat.

Weather and seasons. Continuous rain, heat or cold are experienced unfiltered in the van. Winter presents many with a choice: south, a permanent parking space or back into an apartment.

bureaucracy. Registration address, health insurance, post office, banking, possibly business - anyone who lives permanently mobile has to solve these questions properly.

Loneliness and Uprooting. As warm as the community is, friendships on the road often remain fleeting, and family and old friends are far away. Not everyone can tolerate this in the long run.

Unsafe Internet. For anyone who needs to earn money on the go, poor reception is more than a nuisance - it is a business risk.

How to find out whether vanlife is right for you

The mistake many beginners make: put everything on one card - cancel your apartment, buy a van, drive off. The step-by-step test is wiser. Initially spend two to four weeks at a time in the vehicle, ideally even in bad weather. Try working remotely for a few days and see whether your employer or customers will support it in the long term. Calculate your costs realistically. And keep a retreat option at the beginning - an apartment, a room with friends, a subtenancy.

Many end up with a hybrid model: a fixed basis plus several months of travel per year. This is not a failed van life, but for many simply the best version of it.

Conclusion

Vanlife is a lifestyle with real freedom - and real costs in the form of logistics, sacrifice of comfort and social compromises. The beautiful pictures are true, but they only show half the truth. If you start with realistic expectations, clarify your work and income situation in advance and start small instead of burning everything, you have the best chance of the van not just remaining an experiment, but becoming a home.
