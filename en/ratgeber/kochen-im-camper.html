Cooking in the camper: good food on two burners

Guide · 7 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

The camper's kitchen rewards simplicity: a few, good utensils instead of full equipment, one-pot dishes as standard, fresh and regionally purchased at markets. When the weather is good, the kitchen moves outside (less heat, smell and humidity in the van), washing up is done using the two-bowl method and biodegradable dishwashing liquid. Cooking with a lid saves gas - and a fire blanket should be within easy reach.

Two burners, thirty centimeters of work space, a refrigerator the size of a minibar - and yet I swear to you: I often eat better when I'm on the go than at home. No contradiction! The camper kitchen forces simplicity, and simplicity is the basis of good cooking. Here I'll show you how to organize your on-board kitchen, what really works on the go and what tricks you can use to turn little equipment into a lot of enjoyment. 💛

Understanding the on-board kitchen: what it can and cannot do

A typical camper kitchen offers two to three gas burners, a small sink, little work space and a refrigerator between 60 and 140 liters. An oven is the exception, the microwave is a luxury. There are also three silent players that influence every cooking decision: the limited water supply, the limited Gas supply and the fact that cooking fumes remain in the small interior - as heat, smell and moisture.

This results in the three basic rules of the camper kitchen: Cook simply (few pots, few ingredients), cook with a lid (saves gas and reduces steam) and cook outside whenever possible.

The right equipment: fewer, but better

The experience of thousands of campers can be boiled down to a short list: a large and a small pot, a good non-stick pan, a sharp knife, a sturdy cutting board, spatula, wooden spoon, sieve and a bowl that is suitable for mixing, serving and washing up. Plus stackable storage containers and reusable bags.

Three extras deserve their place: a Omnia oven or comparable top oven for the gas flame - it bakes bread, casseroles and cakes and expands the repertoire enormously; a Bialetti or a hand filter for real coffee; and a Outdoor cooker or compact gas grill for the open-air kitchen. What, on the other hand, almost always goes unused: special appliances, the third cutting board and dishes “for guests” who never come. 😄

One-Pot: the principle of success

Dishes from one pot are the kings of the camper kitchen: one flame, minimal washing up, maximum taste. The principle: fry onions and vegetables, add liquid and starch side dish (pasta, rice, couscous, lentils) directly, cook with the lid on, season to taste, and you're done. On this basis, countless variations are created - Mediterranean with tomatoes and olives, oriental with chickpeas and cumin, Asian with coconut milk and curry.

Also tried and tested: stir-fries with what the local market has to offer, shakshuka for breakfast or dinner, wraps and bowls made from prepared components, soups in the cold season and for the weekend: fresh bread from the top oven - the smell alone is worth the device.

Shop regionally: the pantry is called a travel destination

Perhaps the most beautiful aspect of the camper kitchen: your pantry changes with each destination. Weekly markets, farm shops and fish stalls provide ingredients that are fresher and often cheaper than any supermarket - and the same old pasta dish becomes a new travel experience every time with local vegetables, cheese or fish. In Poland I regularly went to the weekly markets - the pierogi ingredients alone are worth the trip. 😉

So plan a little in advance: A basic stock of non-perishables travels with you (pasta, rice, pureed tomatoes, oil, spices, canned goods for emergencies), everything fresh is bought on site every two or three days. This also keeps the refrigerator organized - important, because a full refrigerator cools poorly, and with absorption devices the following also applies: you need a vehicle that is fairly horizontal in order to provide performance.

Cooking outside: the summer rule

As soon as the weather permits, the kitchen moves outside. There are solid reasons for this: frying and stewing in the interior heats up the camper, spreads odors into the upholstery and curtains and forms a film of grease and moisture on surfaces. A gas stove on the camping table or a compact grill solves all of this - and makes cooking a sociable part of the evening.

Two restrictions: open fires and sometimes even gas grills are in Banned in many places in dry regions and summer months - inform yourself and respect bans without exception. And when it's windy, the stove should be in the windbreak, otherwise half the gas will be ineffective.

Save water: the dishwashing school

Water is the scarcest resource on board, and washing up is its largest consumer. The tried-and-tested routine: Wipe out plates and pans roughly with kitchen paper immediately after eating (this also prevents grease in the gray water tank), then wash them in a bowl with a little hot soapy water, rinse briefly in a second bowl, and dry. A complete meal for four people requires just a few liters. Biodegradable dishwashing liquid is standard - for the sake of the gray water tank and the environment.

Saving gas works in a similarly unspectacular way: put the lid on, only boil water in the required amount, let the pasta steep with residual heat after boiling.

Security: small, but important

When cooking indoors, always open a window or the roof hatch - the flame consumes oxygen and produces water vapor. Never leave pots unattended, first turn off the stove, then turn off the gas - and of course the flame is off while you are driving. A fire blanket within easy reach of the kitchen is part of the basic equipment.

Conclusion: The camper kitchen rewards everyone who sees simplicity as a strength: a pot, fresh regional ingredients, cooked outside, washed up with little water. Anyone who abandons the idea of ​​copying the kitchen at home will realize that cooking on the go is not the restricted version of cooking - it is the relaxed one. And no cuisine in the world has a better view. 🌅
