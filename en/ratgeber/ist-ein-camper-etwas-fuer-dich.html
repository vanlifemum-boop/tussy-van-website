Is a camper something for you? The honest self-test before the big step

Guide · 7 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

Your own bed that travels with you. Coffee with a mountain view, without hotel breakfast times. The dream of a camper sounds tempting - but is this form of travel really suitable for everyone? Before you invest a lot of money, it's worth taking an honest look at your travel style.

Which one Are you a traveler?

People travel very differently. Some people love to be constantly on the go, discovering a new place every day. Others arrive at a destination and stay there for two weeks. Still others mix both. A camper especially comes into its own if you like to be flexible and mobile: change your route, stay longer spontaneously, continue driving if the weather turns bad.

Who, on the other hand, prefers to Poolside and enjoying all-inclusive, you probably won't be happy with a camper - and that's completely okay.

The biggest advantages

Freedom and independence. You decide the route, pace and length of stay yourself. No check-in, no departure times, no suitcase limits.

Your home travels with you. Your own bed, your own kitchen, your own toilet and shower - everywhere. It's worth its weight in gold, especially with children or pets.

Cost control on long trips. Cook yourself instead of a restaurant, parking space instead of a hotel: If you are on the road for longer, you can save significantly.

Proximity to nature. Waking up at the lake, sunset in front of the door - experiences that no hotel room can offer.

The honest disadvantages

Costs. Purchase, insurance, maintenance, fuel, parking spaces, tolls - a camper is not a bargain. You should also take the depreciation into account.

Space and comfort. In just a few square meters, life is different than at home. Water and electricity supplies are limited, it can be hot in summer and cold in winter.

Effort. Supply and disposal, vehicle maintenance, finding a parking space - a camper wants to be "served". And driving a larger vehicle takes some practice.

Downtimes. Most campers sit unused for most of the year - and need a place to park.

The smartest first step: renting instead of buying

The best reality check is a rental trip. Rent a camper for one to two weeks and try out everyday life: cooking on a low flame, filling the water tank, emptying the toilet cassette, living together in a small space. Only then will you really know whether camper life suits you - and what vehicle size and equipment you need.

Many people also notice what when renting What is important to them: a fixed bed instead of a converted seating area, a larger kitchen, more storage space. These findings are priceless when purchasing later.

Conclusion

A camper is not a vehicle, but a travel philosophy Love camper life. If you value luxury, service and zero hassle, it's better to stay at a hotel.

Our tip: rent first, then decide. This is how you can find out without risk whether the dream of a rolling home is also your dream.

Instagram is full of them: camper with the back door open on the beach, steaming coffee in your hand, the sea behind. It's no wonder that more and more people dream of having their own rolling home. But between the beautiful picture and reality lie toilet cassettes, searching for a parking space in the rain and the question of what to do with 30,000 to 100,000 euros. Before you take this step, you should ask yourself an honest question: Does camper life suit me at all?

Travel styles are different - and that's a good thing

There is no one right way to travel. Some people want to see as much as possible and prefer to move on every day. Others look for a nice place and stay there for the entire vacation. Many are somewhere in between. A camper is the perfect solution for anyone who wants to be mobile and flexible: mountains today, sea tomorrow, and if it's particularly beautiful somewhere, you simply stay longer.

You often only find your travel style by trying it out. Anyone who has already traveled with package tours, rental car tours, tents or holiday apartments knows the weaknesses of each option: with an organized trip there is no freedom of choice, with a pure car trip there is no shower and toilet, and with camping there is a lack of comfort and a feeling of security. The camper fills exactly this gap - it combines mobility with a minimum of living comfort.

The advantages: What the camper can really do

Maximum freedom. You decide for yourself the route, pace and stages. If you like a place, you stay. If it rains, you follow the sun. No hotel check-out at 11 a.m., no flight to catch.

Your home is always there. Your own bed with your own bed linen, your own kitchen, your own bathroom. You never have to pack and unpack your suitcase again - everything has its own place. This is a huge advantage for families with small children and those traveling with dogs: familiar surroundings, familiar rhythm, no matter where you are.

Cost advantage on long journeys. The purchase is expensive, no question. But you save on every overnight stay and every home-cooked meal along the way. Especially when traveling for several weeks or with the whole family, this quickly pays off compared to a hotel plus restaurant.

Pure nature experience. Waking up with a view of the lake, the starry sky in front of your door in the evening - a camper takes you to places and moments that no hotel room can offer.

Spontaneity. Weekend trip? Just get in and drive off. The camper is packed and ready, the basic equipment remains on board.

The disadvantages: What no one shows you on Instagram

The costs don't stop after the purchase. Insurance, taxes, maintenance, tires, MOT, gas test, winter parking space, fuel on the way, tolls, ferries and parking space fees. Added to this is the loss of value. A camper is a hobby with ongoing costs - calculate realistically before you sign.

Living in a small space. A camper rarely has more than 10-15 square meters of living space. This means: little privacy, compromises on storage space and the need to be tidy sinks, you will despair in the camper.

Limited resources. Water, electricity and gas are finite. You quickly learn to manage with 100 liters of fresh water and wonder where the nearest disposal station is. That's part of the adventure - but also work.

Driving needs to be learned. A seven meter long, three meter high vehicle drives differently than a car. Narrow old towns, low bridges, finding a parking space - all of this requires attention and initially also nerves.

Dependence on the weather. Two weeks of constant rain in 12 square meters with two children? This is a stress test for every family. A plan B is always part of it.

Standing time. Most campers are only used for a few weeks a year. The rest of the time they need a place to park and regular care so that moisture and standing damage have no chance.

The practical test: rent before you buy

There's an easy way to answer all of these questions without risking a fortune: rent a camper. One to two weeks is enough to get to know everyday life - cooking on two burners, washing up with little water, emptying the toilet cassette, living together in a small space, reversing into parking at the campsite.

When renting, you also learn which vehicle size and layout suits you. Do you need a permanent bed or is the converted seating area enough? How important is a large kitchen to you? Can you handle a compact van or do you need a full-fledged motorhome? These findings are worth money when you buy it later, because a bad purchase is expensive.

Ideally, you even rent twice: once a smaller vehicle and once a larger vehicle, preferably at different times of the year. This is how you get a realistic picture.

Who is a camper the right choice for?

A camper is right for you if you:

  • Freedom and flexibility are valued more highly than luxury and service
  • gladly are in nature and want to discover new things
  • you are ready to take care of your vehicle
  • can live with limited space and simple comfort
  • See travel as an active experience, not as just resting

A camper is probably not right for you if you primarily want to be served on holiday, value a lot of space and luxury or if you value any form of Want to avoid "work" on vacation.

Conclusion: First test, then dream, then buy

The camper is more than a vehicle - it is a decision for a certain way of traveling and living. For some it is the greatest freedom, for others it is an expensive misunderstanding. The difference is rarely due to the vehicle, but almost always to the expectations.

Therefore our advice: Be honest with yourself, rent first, talk to experienced campers and only make the purchase decision once you have experienced real camper life. If you're still excited, there's nothing standing in the way of your home on wheels - and neither is a life full of travel freedom.
