🧭 Buying advice

Is a camper right for you? The honest self-test before the big step

Guide · 7 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here is the summary

A separate bed that comes along. Coffee with a mountain view, no hotel breakfast times. The dream of a camper sounds tempting – but is this type of travel really suitable for everyone? Before investing a lot of money, it's worth taking an honest look at your travel style.

What kind of traveler are you?

People travel very differently. Some people love being constantly on the move, discovering a new place every day. Others arrive at a destination and stay there for two weeks. Others mix both. A camper plays to its strengths especially when you like to be flexible and mobile: change route, spontaneously stay longer, keep driving when the weather changes.

On the other hand, those who prefer to lie by the pool and enjoy all-inclusive will probably not be happy with a camper – and that's perfectly fine.

The biggest advantages

Freedom and independence. You decide the route, pace, and length of stay yourself. No check-in, no departure times, no luggage limits.

Your home travels with you. Private bed, private kitchen, private toilet and shower – everywhere. Especially with children or pets, it's worth its weight in gold.

Cost control on long journeys. Cook for yourself instead of a restaurant, a parking space instead of a hotel: Those who are traveling longer can save significantly.

Close to nature. Waking up by the lake, sunset at your doorstep – experiences no hotel room offers.

The honest drawbacks

Costs. Purchase, insurance, maintenance, fuel, pitches, tolls – a camper is no bargain. You should also factor in depreciation.

Space and comfort. Living on just a few square meters is different from home. Water and electricity supplies are limited; it can get hot in summer and cold in winter.

Effort. Supply and disposal, vehicle maintenance, finding a parking spot – a camper wants to be "served." And driving a larger vehicle takes some practice.

Idle times. Most campers spend most of the year unused – and need a parking spot.

The smartest first step: Rent instead of buy

The best reality check is a rental trip. Rent a camper for one to two weeks and try out everyday life: cooking on a low flame, filling the water tank, emptying the toilet cassette, living together in a small space. Only then do you really know if camper life suits you – and what vehicle size and equipment you need.

Many also realize when renting what is important to them: a fixed bed instead of a convertible seating area, a larger kitchen, more storage space. These insights are priceless when purchased later.

Conclusion

A camper is not a vehicle, but a travel philosophy. If you're looking for freedom, flexibility, and closeness to nature, and are willing to trade some comfort and convenience for it, you'll love the camper life. If you value luxury, service, and zero effort, it's better to stick with the hotel.

Our tip: Rent first, then decide. This way, you can safely find out whether the dream of a rolling home is actually your dream.

Instagram is full of them: campers with the back door open on the beach, steaming coffee in hand, the sea behind them. No wonder more and more people dream of having their own rolling home. But between the beautiful picture and reality lie toilet cassettes, searching for a parking spot in the rain, and the question of where to put 30,000 to 100,000 euros. Before you take this step, you should ask yourself an honest question: Does camper life even suit me?

Travel styles are different – and that's a good thing

There is no one right way to travel. Some people want to see as much as possible and prefer to move on every day. Others look for a nice place and stay there for the entire holiday. Many fall somewhere in between. A camper is the perfect solution for anyone who wants to be mobile and flexible: mountains today, sea tomorrow, and if it's especially beautiful somewhere, you simply stay longer.

Often, you only discover your travel style by trying it out. Anyone who has already traveled with package tours, rental car tours, tents, or holiday apartments knows the weaknesses of each option: Organized trips lack freedom of choice, pure car trips lack showers and toilets, and camping lacks comfort and a sense of security. This is exactly the gap the camper falls into – it combines mobility with a minimum level of living comfort.

The advantages: What the camper can really do

Maximum freedom. You decide the route, pace, and stages yourself. If you like a place, you stay. If it rains, you follow the sun. No hotel check-out at 11 a.m., no flight you have to catch.

Your home is always there. Private bed with own linens, own kitchen, private bathroom. You'll never have to pack and unpack your suitcase again – everything has its fixed place. For families with small children and for travelers with dogs, this is a huge advantage: familiar surroundings, familiar rhythm, no matter where you are.

Cost advantage on long journeys. The purchase is expensive, no question. But along the way, you save on every overnight stay and every meal you cook. Especially for multi-week trips or with the whole family, this quickly pays off compared to hotel plus restaurant.

Pure nature experience. Waking up with a view of the lake, the starry sky at your door in the evening – a camper takes you to places and moments that no hotel room can offer.

Spontaneity. Weekend trip? Just hop in and set off. The camper is fully packed, and the basic equipment stays on board.

The Disadvantages: What No One Shows You on Instagram

The costs do not stop after purchase. Insurance, taxes, maintenance, tires, TÜV, gas inspection, winter parking, fuel, tolls, ferries, and parking fees. Added to this is the loss of value. A camper is a hobby with ongoing costs – calculate realistically before you sign.

Living in a small space. A camper rarely has more than 10–15 square meters of living space. That means: little privacy, compromises on storage space, and the need to be tidy. Anyone who gets lost in chaos at home will despair in the camper.

Limited resources. Water, electricity, and gas are finite. You quickly learn to hold on to 100 liters of fresh water and to ask yourself where the next disposal station is. That's part of the adventure – but also work.

Driving requires learning. A seven-meter-long, three-meter-high vehicle drives differently than a car. Narrow old towns, low bridges, searching for parking – all of this requires attention and, at first, nerves.

Weather dependence. Two weeks of continuous rain on 12 square meters with two children? This is a strain for every family. A plan B is always part of it.

Durability. Most campers are only used for a few weeks each year. The rest of the time, they need a parking space and regular care so that moisture and standing damage have no chance.

The practical test: Rent before you buy

There is an easy way to answer all these questions without risking a fortune: rent a campervan. One to two weeks is enough to get to know everyday life – cooking on two burners, washing dishes with little water, emptying the toilet cassette, living together in a tight space, parking in reverse at the campsite.

When renting, you also learn which vehicle size and layout suits you. Do you need a fixed bed, or is the converted seating area sufficient? How important is a large kitchen to you? Can you manage with a compact panel van or do you need a full-fledged motorhome? These insights are worth their money later on, because a bad purchase is costly.

Ideally, you even rent twice: once a smaller vehicle and once a larger vehicle, preferably at different times of the year. This way, you get a realistic picture.

Who is a camper the right choice for?

A camper suits you if you:

  • Values freedom and flexibility more than luxury and service
  • enjoy being in nature and want to discover new things
  • are ready to take care of your vehicle
  • can live with limited space and simple comfort
  • Understand travel as an active experience, not as pure rest

A camper is probably not right for you if you mainly want to be served on vacation, value plenty of space and luxury, or want to avoid any kind of "work" on vacation.

Conclusion: Test first, then dream, then buy

The camper is more than a vehicle – it is a choice for a specific way of traveling and living. For some, it is the greatest freedom; for others, an expensive misunderstanding. The difference rarely lies in the vehicle, but almost always in expectations.

That's why our advice: Be honest with yourself, rent first, talk to experienced campers, and only make your purchase decision once you've experienced real camper life. If you're still fired up, nothing stands in the way of your rolling home anymore—and not a life full of freedom to travel.
