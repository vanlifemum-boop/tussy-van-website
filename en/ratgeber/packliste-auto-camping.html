Packing list for car camping: This is what you really need to sleep in the car

Guide · 5 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

Four areas decide: Sleeping (folding mattress or sleeping mat, warm sleeping bag, pillow, thermal mats for blackout), Kitchen (single-burner stove, minimal dishes, 10-15 liter water canister), Electricity & Light (Power bank or power station, headlamp, LED battery lamps - the car battery remains taboo) and Hygiene (first aid kit, wet wipes, toilet paper plus a small shovel, tear-resistant garbage bags). The most important rule: Less is more!

You don't need a motorhome that costs tens of thousands of euros to experience the ultimate feeling of freedom on the road. Your own everyday car, a bit of a sense of adventure and, above all, the right equipment are often enough. If you pack cleverly, you can transform even a compact station wagon or SUV into a cozy base camp. But what is really essential on a road trip - and what just blocks valuable storage space? Here's your packing list for sleeping in the car. 💛

By the way: We clarify in my guide whether the car is even enough for you or whether it should be a camper. "Car or camper?"And if you're traveling with a real camper, there's here is the big camper packing list.

1. Sleep - the foundation of every successful trip

A bad night's sleep can ruin the most beautiful route. Since space in the car is limited and seats rarely form a perfectly flat surface, you shouldn't make any compromises here:

  • Mattress, sleeping pad or folding mattress: A tailor-made foam folding mattress offers the best comfort. Alternatively, thick, self-inflating camping sleeping pads are extremely space-saving to transport.
  • The right sleeping bag or blanket: Pay careful attention to the comfort temperature of the sleeping bag. Nights in the car can be surprisingly cold even in summer, as sheet metal and glass hardly insulate.
  • A good pillow: Never underestimate the value of an ergonomic pillow. A small, handy pillow provides the necessary feel-good factor.
  • Blackouts and window curtains: An absolute must-have. Custom-fit thermal mats with suction cups kill two birds with one stone: They protect against prying eyes and at the same time insulate against heat or cold.

2. The mobile kitchen - culinary freedom in the smallest of spaces

The smell of fresh coffee in the morning right after waking up is priceless. Your on-board kitchen should be compact, functional and easy to clean:

  • Gas cooker or cartridge burner: A simple, sturdy single-burner camping stove is sufficient for preparing quick meals, soups or coffee.
  • Minimalist tableware set: A pot, a pan, a sharp knife as well as deep plates and Cutlery made of robust material (e.g. enamel or stainless steel).
  • Generous water supply: Always carry a sturdy water canister (at least 10-15 liters) of fresh water for cooking, washing up and washing your hands.
Important after Camping etiquette: Cook inconspicuously, never leave leftover food that could attract wild animals, and always take your garbage with you. You can find all the unwritten rules in my Camping etiquette →

3. Electricity and light - independence away from sockets

As soon as the engine is off, you should protect the car battery so as not to experience a nasty surprise the next morning. Instead, rely on self-sufficient energy sources:

  • Power bank or portable power station: A compact power bank charges your smartphone. If you want to run a cool box or laptop, it's best to use a small power station (preferably with a solar panel).
  • Headlamp: Probably the most practical helper of all. If you have to look for something outside the car in the dark or arrive late, you always have both hands free.
  • LED interior lighting: Small, dimmable LED battery lamps or fairy lights provide cozy, warm light inside the vehicle without putting a strain on the car battery.

4. Safety and hygiene - clean and relaxed on the go

When standing wild or on simple parking spaces, there are usually no sanitary facilities. Good preparation protects the environment and ensures well-being:

  • Extensive Travel first aid kit: In addition to plasters and bandages, your luggage should include painkillers, mosquito repellent, wound ointment and your personal medication.
  • Smart hygiene aids: Wet wipes and biodegradable Dry shampoo is a lifesaver when there's no shower in sight for days.
  • Toilet paper and a small shovel: If an emergency forces you into the great outdoors, the top rule applies: leave no trace. Bury your leftovers deep enough in the ground.
  • Tear-resistant garbage bags: Waste separation also works on the go. Consistently collect everything in the car until you can throw the waste at an official disposal station.
Conclusion for your adventure: The most important rule when Car camping is: Less is more! Every item that you pack too much, you have to put away from left to right in the narrow interior. Focus on quality and multifunctionality - and enjoy the uncomplicated spontaneity that this type of travel brings with it.
