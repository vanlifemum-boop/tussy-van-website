Packing list for car camping: What you really need to sleep in a car

Guide · 5 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here is the summary

Four areas decide: Sleep (Folding mattress or sleeping pad, warm sleeping bag, pillows, thermal mats for darkening), Kitchen (single-burner stove, minimal dishes, 10–15 liter water canister), Power & Light (Power bank or power station, headlamp, LED battery lamps — car batteries remain off-limits) and Hygiene (Travel first aid kit, wet wipes, toilet paper plus small shovel, tear-resistant garbage bags). The most important rule: less is more!

You don't need a motorhome costing several tens of thousands of euros to experience the ultimate feeling of freedom on the road. Often, your own everyday car, a bit of adventurousness, and above all, the right equipment are enough. Those who pack smart can even turn a compact station wagon or SUV into a cozy basecamp. But what is truly indispensable on a road trip — and what only blocks valuable storage space? Here is your packing list for sleeping in the car. 💛

By the way: Whether the car is even enough for you or if you want a camper after all, we'll clarify in my guide "Car or camper?". And if you're traveling with a real camper, there are here is the big camper packing list.

1. Sleep — the foundation of every successful trip

Poor sleep can ruin the most beautiful route. Since space in the car is limited and seats rarely form a perfectly flat surface, you shouldn't compromise here:

  • Mattress, sleeping pad, or folding mattress: A custom foam folding mattress offers the best comfort. Alternatively, thick, self-inflating camping sleeping mats are extremely space-saving to transport.
  • The right sleeping bag or blanket: Pay close attention to the comfort temperature of the sleeping bag. Nights in the car can even get surprisingly cold in summer, since sheet metal and glass hardly insulate.
  • A good pillow: Never underestimate the value of an ergonomic pillow. A small, handy pillow provides the necessary feel-good factor.
  • Blackout and window curtains: An absolute must-have. Precisely fitting thermal mats with suction cups kill two birds with one stone: they protect against prying eyes and at the same time insulate against heat or cold.

2. The mobile kitchen — culinary freedom in the smallest space

The smell of fresh coffee in the morning right after waking up is priceless. Your onboard kitchen should be compact, functional, and easy to clean:

  • Gas stove or cartridge burner: A simple, sturdy single-flame camping stove is more than enough to prepare quick meals, soups, or coffee.
  • Minimalist tableware set: A pot, a pan, a sharp knife, as well as deep plates and cutlery made of sturdy material (e.g., enamel or stainless steel).
  • Generous water supply: Always carry a sturdy water canister (at least 10–15 liters) with fresh water for cooking, rinsing, and handwashing.
Important according to camping etiquette: Cook discreetly, never leave food scraps that could attract wildlife, and take your trash back without exception. You can find all the unwritten rules in my Camping etiquette →

3. Electricity and light — independence away from power outlets

Once the engine is off, you should conserve the car battery to avoid an unpleasant surprise the next morning. Instead, rely on self-sufficient energy sources:

  • Power bank or portable power station: A compact power bank charges your smartphone. If you want to operate a cooler or laptop, it's best to use a small power station (preferably with a solar panel).
  • Headlamp: Probably the most practical helper ever. If you need to look for something outside the car in the dark or arrive late, you always have both hands free.
  • LED interior lighting: Small, dimmable LED battery lamps or string lights provide cozy, warm light inside the vehicle without burdening the car battery.

4. Safety and hygiene — clean and relaxed on the go

When standing wild or on simple pitches, there are usually no sanitary facilities. Good preparation protects the environment and ensures well-being:

  • Extensive travel first aid kit: In addition to plasters and bandages, painkillers, mosquito repellent, wound ointment, and your personal medications should be included.
  • Smart hygiene aids: Wet wipes and biodegradable dry shampoo are a lifesaver when there's no shower in sight for days.
  • Toilet paper and a small scoop: If an emergency drives you into the great outdoors, the top rule applies: leave no traces. Bury your remains deep enough in the ground.
  • Tear-resistant garbage bags: Waste separation also works on the go. Consistently collect everything in the car until you can drop off the waste at an official disposal station.
Conclusion for your adventure: The most important rule in car camping is: Less is more! Any part you overpack must be cleared from left to right in the tight interior space. Focus on quality and multifunctionality — and enjoy the straightforward spontaneity that this way of traveling brings.
