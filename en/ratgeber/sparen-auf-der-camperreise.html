Save on the Camper trip: the big budget guide

Guide · 8 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

The biggest levers in order: Sprit (drive at a speed of 100-110 instead of 130, do fewer kilometers, refuel via the app, clear out the weight), Overnight stays (Mix of pitches, free standing and campsite as a service stop; low season and discount cards like ACSI), Food (on-board kitchen instead of restaurant, markets instead Camping shop), Tolls & ferries (check toll-free routes, book early) and Buy used equipment. The nice thing: Almost all savings tips make the trip better instead of worse.

The camper is considered a cheap form of travel - and can still cost a lot of money: diesel, tolls, campsites in high season, plus ice cream here and excursions there. The good news: Hardly any form of travel can be optimized so effectively. If you know where the big cost blocks are, you will travel twice as long with the same budget - I speak from years of experience. 😉 Here is my complete savings guide, sorted by effect.

Lever 1: Fuel - this is where the most money is

On most trips, fuel is the largest single item. Three strategies work immediately:

Drive slower. The air resistance increases with the square of the speed - in a tall, box-shaped vehicle this has a big impact. Depending on the vehicle, a speed of 100-110 instead of 130 saves one to two liters per 100 kilometers, is easier on your nerves and makes the journey more relaxing. Over 3,000 kilometers that's easily 50 to 100 euros.

Drive less. The most honest savings tip: kilometers that you don't drive don't cost anything. Exploring a region intensively is almost always cheaper and more relaxing than exploring half of Europe. Plan standing days - they save money and are usually the best days of the trip.

Refuel smartly. Tank apps show the cheapest stations in the area; Motorway gas stations are the most expensive almost everywhere. It's worth comparing prices before national borders: there are sometimes significant differences per liter between neighboring countries - driving across the border with a full or empty tank can mean a noticeable amount with large tanks.

There is also the topic of Weight: Every unnecessary kilo increases consumption. Anyone who consistently clears out after the first season saves twice - on fuel and on their nerves.

Lever 2: Overnight stays - the right mix

If you stay at the four-star campsite every night, you can easily pay three figures per day with your family in the high season. The alternative is the rotation mix:

  • Pitches (often 5-20 euros, sometimes free) for most nights.
  • Free standingwhere it is allowed - free of charge and often the most beautiful (for Poland I have you written here where it is legal.)
  • Camping every few days as a service stop: do laundry, stock up on tanks, take a long shower.

Other savings levers: Low season travel - May, June and September often offer better weather than midsummer, half as full seats and significantly lower prices. Discount systems such as ACSI card or Camping Key Europe often pay for themselves after just a few nights. For longer stays in Weekly or long-term prices Ask - many places give decent discounts. And parking space concepts at wineries and farms combine free overnight stays with regional enjoyment.

Lever 3: Catering - the on-board kitchen as a money box

Having your own kitchen is the camper's built-in savings trick. Those who do most of their cooking themselves spend hardly more than they would at home - while a family of four can easily spend a high double-digit to three-digit sum in a restaurant each evening. The practice:

  • Local and seasonal shopping: Frequent markets and discount stores instead of tourist supermarkets at the campsite (whose prices are often steep).
  • Meal-Prep light: Take basic ingredients from home with you, only add fresh things on the go.
  • Coffee and snacks on board: The motorway cappuccino and the rest stop fries are the silent budget killers of every trip.
  • Restaurant as an experience, not as an embarrassment: Consciously planned visits to local restaurants are part of traveling - they are more special and cheaper than the daily "Oh, let's not cook."

You can find more kitchen tricks in my guide "Cooking in the camper" →

Lever 4: Tolls, ferries, routes

Toll costs are often underestimated: a tour of southern Europe can add up to several hundred euros. Savings approaches:

  • Check toll-free routes: The country road is often not only cheaper, but also the nicer route - you have time in the camper.
  • Buy vignettes digitally and in good timeavoid double purchases and penalties.
  • Know weight classes: In some countries, vehicles over 3.5 tons pay significantly more More reason to keep an eye on weight.
  • Book ferries early and specify the vehicle length correctly - early bookers save a lot, incorrect information becomes expensive at the port.

Lever 5: Equipment and fixed costs

  • Used buy: Camping furniture, awnings, accessories - the used market is huge and the savings are significant. Flea markets on campsites are real treasure troves.
  • Rent instead of buying for things that are only used once (roof box, special tools, snow chains).
  • Compare insurance policies annually, check season license plate if the camper is parked in winter anyway.
  • Do maintenance yourself where it is safe: Maintaining seals, changing water filters, cleaning the interior - workshop hours are expensive. Safety-related matters (gas, brakes) remain with the professionals. For me the motto is anyway: women do their own work. 💪

The saving mindset: conscious instead of stingy

The most important point at the end: Saving when traveling works best as a conscious decision, not as constant sacrifice. A simple expense tracking (note app is enough) shows after a week where the money is going - and usually it's not the planned expenses, but the many small unplanned ones. Anyone who knows them can take countermeasures without making the trip worse.

Conclusion: The great irony of saving money in a camper: Almost all effective tips make the trip better instead of worse. Driving slower is more relaxing. Fewer kilometers mean more experience. Markets are more beautiful than rest stops, the off-season is emptier than August, the parking space at the winery is more charming than plot 134. If you travel consciously, you automatically save - and vice versa.
