Saving on your camper trip: the big budget guide

Guide · 8 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here is the summary

The largest levers in order: Alcohol (Driving 100–110 km/h instead of 130, driving fewer kilometers, refueling via app, clearing out the weight), Overnight stays (Mix of pitches, freestanding, and campsite as a service stop; Low season and discount cards such as ACSI), Food (Onboard kitchen instead of restaurant, markets instead of camping shop), Tolls & Ferries (check toll-free routes, book early) and Buy used equipment. The good news: Almost all saving tips make the trip better rather than worse.

The camper is considered an affordable way to travel — and can still be quite expensive: diesel, tolls, campsites in high season, plus the ice here and the excursion there. The good news: Hardly any other form of travel can be optimized as effectively. Who knows where the major cost blocks lie, traveling twice as long with the same budget — I speak from years of experience. 😉 Here is my complete savings guide, sorted by effect.

Lever 1: Fuel — this is where most of the money lies

On most trips, fuel is the largest single item. Three strategies work immediately:

Drive slower. Air resistance increases with the square of speed — with a tall, box-shaped vehicle, this really kicks in. Speeds of 100–110 instead of 130 save one to two liters per 100 kilometers depending on the vehicle, ease the nerves, and make the drive more relaxing. Over 3,000 kilometers, that's quickly 50 to 100 euros.

Drive less. The most honest money-saving tip: Kilometers you don't drive cost nothing. Exploring a region intensively is almost always cheaper and more relaxing than exploring half of Europe. Schedule rest days — they save money and are usually the best days of the trip.

Think smart. Fuel apps show the cheapest stations in the area; Highway gas stations are the most expensive almost everywhere. At national borders, it's worth comparing prices: there are sometimes significant differences per liter between neighboring countries — crossing the border with a full or empty tank can make a noticeable difference for large tanks.

Added to this is the topic Weight: Every unnecessary kilo increases consumption. Those who consistently clean out after the first season save twice — on fuel and on nerves.

Lever 2: Overnight stays — the right mix

Anyone who stays at the four-star campsite every night quickly pays triple digits per day with family during the high season. The alternative is the rotational mix:

  • Pitches (often 5–20 euros, sometimes free) for most nights.
  • Free Standing, where it is allowed — free and often at its best. (For Poland, I have given you written down here where it is legal.)
  • Campsite every few days as a service stop: washing laundry, supplying tanks, showering thoroughly.

Other savings levers: Low season Travel — May, June, and September often offer better weather than the height of summer, less crowded places, and significantly lower prices. Discount Systems like ACSI-Card or Camping Key Europe, often pay for themselves after just a few nights. For longer stays to Weekly or long-term prices ask — many places offer substantial discounts. And pitch concepts at winemakers and farms combine free overnight stays with regional enjoyment.

Lever 3: Catering — the onboard kitchen as a piggy bank

The camper's own kitchen is the camper's built-in saving trick. Those who mostly cook themselves spend hardly more than at home — while a family of four quickly spends a high double- to three-figure sum per evening in a restaurant. The practice:

  • Shop local and seasonally: Weekly markets and discount stores instead of tourist supermarkets at the campsite (whose prices are often steep).
  • Light Meal-Prep: Take basic ingredients from home with you, add only fresh ones on the go.
  • Coffee and snacks on board: The highway cappuccino and the rest stop fries are the silent budget killers of every trip.
  • Restaurant as an experience, not as an embarrassment: Consciously planned visits to local restaurants are part of traveling — they are more special and cheaper than the daily "Oh, let's not cook."

Lever 4: Toll, Ferries, Routes

Toll costs are often underestimated: on a Southern Europe tour, several hundred euros can accumulate. Savings approaches:

  • Check toll-free routes: The country road is often not only cheaper but also the nicer route — after all, you have time in a camper.
  • Buy vignettes digitally and in time, avoid double purchases and penalty fees.
  • Known weight classes: In some countries, vehicles over 3.5 tons pay significantly more — one more reason to keep an eye on the weight.
  • Book ferries early and indicate the vehicle length correctly — early bookers save a lot, false information becomes expensive at the port.

Lever 5: Equipment and fixed costs

  • Buy used: Camping furniture, awnings, accessories — the used market is huge and the savings are significant. Flea markets at campsites are real treasure troves.
  • Borrow instead of buy for items that are only needed once (roof box, special tools, snow chains).
  • Compare insurance policies annually, check the seasonal license plate if the camper is parked in winter anyway.
  • Do maintenance yourself where it is safe: Maintaining seals, changing water filters, interior cleaning — workshop hours are expensive. Safety-relevant matters (gas, brakes) remain with the professional. For me, the motto is: Woman does the work herself. 💪

The saving mindset: consciously instead of stingy

The most important point at the end: saving on travel works best as a conscious decision, not as a constant sacrifice. A simple expense tracking (a note app is enough) shows after a week where the money is going—and usually, it's not the planned expenses, but the many small unplanned ones. Those who know them can counteract without making the journey poorer.

Conclusion: The great irony of camper savings: Almost all effective tips make the trip better rather than worse. Driving slower is more relaxed. Fewer kilometers mean more experiences. Markets are nicer than rest stops, the low season emptier than August, and the winemaker's parking lot more charming than plot 134. Those who travel consciously automatically save money — and vice versa.
