🚗 Buying advice

Car or camper? When your car is enough - and when it's time for the next step

Guide · 6 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here's the short version

For weekend trips, spontaneous excursions and cheap entry, your normal car is completely sufficient - put your mattress or roof tent in and go. The camper is only worthwhile for longer trips, with family or if you want your own bathroom and kitchen. The golden rule before buying: first test it with your existing car, then rent the model you want - in the fall when the weather is bad, not in the middle of summer. Then you'll know whether the floor plan really fits.

It's a dilemma that almost everyone who has wanderlust is familiar with: Do I really need a motorhome - or is my normal car actually sufficient?

As soon as the desire to travel comes knocking, you automatically scroll through sales platforms, marvel at perfectly developed vanlife content and catch yourself thinking whether you don't need a mobile home on four wheels for the ultimate feeling of freedom. But let’s be honest: Getting started in the camping world doesn’t have to be expensive or complicated. Let's take a pragmatic look at the two worlds so that you can find out which type of traveler you are. On your first camper trip, most people pack as if they were going on an expedition to the end of the world. On the fifth trip, everything fits into half of the cupboards - and still nothing is missing. The difference is experience. So that you don't have to pay dearly for it with overloaded cupboards and forgotten essentials, you'll find the complete packing guide here: sorted by area, with the most important must-haves and the most frequently unnecessary souvenirs. really bist. 💛

Car or SUV: the perfect entry for minimalists

You don't have to dig deep into your pockets to experience adventures. Often the perfect travel base is already in your driveway. A normal station wagon, a high-roof station wagon or an SUV are real secret weapons for your vacation.

For whom Is traveling by car ideal?

  • Weekend trips: just drive off on Friday after work and be back on Sunday evening. It couldn't be simpler.
  • Spontaneous decisions: The weather on Lake Garda is better at the weekend than in the Harz? By car you can change your plans in a matter of seconds.
  • Low costs: No expensive additional vehicle to purchase, no extra MOT, no increased toll fees and significantly more relaxed fuel consumption.
  • Easy parking & no stress: Whether it's a parking garage in the city center, narrow serpentines or the parking bay on the beach - with the car you fit in everywhere and (important when it comes to free standing) you hardly stand out as a camper.

For many, sleeping in the car - pragmatically with a suitable mattress in the trunk or a cool roof tent - is the ideal start. You don't need any loans, you don't have to make any major commitments and you can first test whether life on the road suits you at all. The motorhome: when the camper really makes sense

At some point there may come a point where the air mattress in the back is no longer enough. A fully-fledged motorhome or a converted van takes traveling to a completely different level of comfort.

When is it worth taking the step towards a camper?

  • Longer trips: If you're on the road for weeks or months, the space in the car quickly becomes too tight.
  • Traveling with the family: As soon as there are more than two people (or a large four-legged friend) on board, a camper with fixed seating and sleeping places is the salvation of family peace.
  • The need for a bathroom and a permanent kitchen: Having your own kitchen, where you can stand and cook even when it rains, is pure luxury. Even more important: Having your own toilet and shower on board makes you completely self-sufficient.
  • Real life on the move: When the vehicle is not just a means of transport, but your real temporary home. (Like my blue T4 for me. 💙)

But be careful: A camper not only brings comfort, but also a lot of responsibility. You drive a large, heavy vehicle, you have to take care of supply and disposal (keyword: chemical toilet and gray water - here I'll show you where it works in Poland) and should know the unwritten rules of the camping community: the Camping etiquette. Respect for nature and locals is the be-all and end-all.

The best advice at the end: first test, then buy!

Before you start poring through dealer catalogs: Stop.

Don't start by buying a camper, start with the trip!

Take the car you currently have, pack a cooler bag, a sleeping bag and a few basics and set off for a few days. Take a close look: What are you really missing? Is it the place? Do you miss your own toilet? Or is the simplicity actually enough for you?

The ultimate endurance test: the "autumn rental"

If you are absolutely sure that it should be a camper, the golden rule is: Rent first! Borrow exactly the model you have in mind and go away for a week. And now comes the most important trick: Don't do that in midsummer!

In July and August, life takes place outside, you sit under the awning and only use the interior for sleeping. Every camper seems huge. Drive instead in autumnwhen it's cold outside, the wind is whistling and it's raining for days. If you're stuck in the camper with your family or partner for three days, you'll immediately notice whether the floor plan fits, whether there's enough space and whether you feel comfortable in a small space. This saves you expensive bad purchases!

Conclusion: It's about the journey, not that Equipment. No matter whether you end up choosing the flexible SUV, the stylish van or the comfortable motorhome: in the end, it doesn't matter how much your vehicle cost or how big it is. What counts is the journey, switching off from everyday life and the experiences you collect. So: pack your bags and just go!
