🚗 Buying advice

Car or camper? When your car is enough — and when it's time for the next step

Guide · 6 min. reading time

⏱️ No time? Here is the summary

For weekend trips, spontaneous outings, and an affordable start, your regular car is more than enough — mattress or roof tent in and off. The camper is only worthwhile for longer trips, with family, or if you want your own bathroom and a solid kitchen. The golden rule before buying: first test with your existing car, then rent your desired model — in autumn when the weather is bad, not in midsummer. Then you'll know if the floor plan really fits.

It's a dilemma that almost everyone caught by wanderlust knows: Do I really need an RV — or is my regular car completely sufficient?

As soon as the urge to travel knocks, you automatically scroll through sales platforms, marvel at perfectly developed Vanlife content, and find yourself wondering if you might need a mobile home on four wheels for the ultimate feeling of freedom. But hand on heart: Getting started in the world of camping doesn't have to be expensive or complicated. Let's take a very pragmatic look at both worlds so you can find out which type of travel you really are. 💛

Car or SUV: the perfect entry point for minimalists

You don't have to dig deep into your pocket to experience adventure. Often, the perfect travel base is already in your driveway. A regular station wagon, a high-roof wagon, or an SUV are real secret weapons for vacations.

Who is traveling by car ideal for?

  • Weekend trips: Just leave after work on Friday and be back on Sunday evening. It doesn't get any simpler than this.
  • Spontaneous decisions: Is the weather at Lake Garda better on weekends than in the Harz? By car, you can change your plans in seconds.
  • Low cost: No expensive additional vehicle to purchase, no extra TÜV, no increased toll fees, and a much more relaxed fuel consumption.
  • Easy parking & no stress: Whether it's a parking garage downtown, tight switchbacks, or the parking bay on the beach — with your car you can fit in everywhere and (important when it comes to standing freely) hardly stand out as a camper.

For many, sleeping in the car — pragmatically with a matching mattress in the trunk or a cool roof tent — is the ideal start. You don't need loans, don't take on big commitments, and first test whether life "on the road" suits you at all. What you should pack for that is in my Packing list for car camping →

The motorhome: when the camper really makes sense

At some point, there might come a point when the air mattress in the rear is no longer enough. A full-fledged motorhome or a converted panel van takes travel to a whole new level of comfort.

When is it worth taking the step to camper?

  • Longer trips: If you're on the road for weeks or months, the space in the car quickly becomes too tight.
  • Traveling with the family: As soon as more than two people (or one large four-legged friend) are on board, a camper with fixed seating and sleeping places is the lifesaver for family peace.
  • The need for a bathroom and a solid kitchen: Having your own kitchen, where you can stand and cook even in the rain, is pure luxury. Even more importantly: a private toilet and a shower on board make you completely self-sufficient.
  • Real life on the road: If the vehicle is to be not just a means of transportation, but your real temporary home. (Just like my blue T4 did for me.) 💙

But beware: A camper not only brings comfort but also a lot of responsibility. You drive a large, heavy vehicle and have to take care of supply and disposal (keyword chemical toilet and greywater — here I'll show you where that works in Poland) and should know the unwritten rules of the camping community: the Camping etiquette. Respect for nature and locals is paramount.

The best advice to finish: test first, then buy!

Before you start browsing dealer catalogs now: Stop.

Don't start by buying a camper. Start with the journey!

Take the car you currently have, pack a cooler bag, a sleeping bag, and some basics, and set off for a few days. Take a very close look: What are you really missing? Is it the place? Do you miss having your own toilet? Or is simplicity really enough for you?

The ultimate tough test: the "autumn rental"

If you're absolutely sure it should be a camper, the golden rule is: Rent first! Borrow exactly the model you have your eye on and take it away for a week. And now comes the most important trick: Don't do that in the height of summer!

In July and August, life takes place outside; you sit under the awning and use the interior only for sleeping. Every camper looks huge there. Drive instead in autumn, when it's cold outside, the wind is blowing, and it rains for days. When you spend three days in a camper with family or a partner, you'll immediately notice whether the layout fits, if the space is sufficient, and if you feel comfortable in a confined space. This saves you costly bad purchases!

Conclusion: It's about the path, not the equipment. No matter whether you end up choosing the flexible SUV, the stylish van, or the comfortable motorhome: in the end, it doesn't matter how much your vehicle cost or how big it is. What counts is the journey, the disconnect from everyday life, and the experiences you gather. So: Pack your bags and just get going!
